## The owner's answers (2026-10-29)

- **Plume at altitude:** "The engine plume SHOULD be faint, but it just seems odd that it is such a dull grey color. Make an assumption based on fuel type if unsure." Faint is right; grey is not. The colour comes from the propellant: kerolox glows orange-yellow from hot soot (its emission, not grey scattering), hydrolox is near-invisible with a faint blue-violet, hypergolics a pale orange-pink. Where the data is missing, take the fuel type's value and mark it (E).
- **Flickers:** "The flickers happen when landed on the launchpad." Reproduce on the pad (Falcon 9, landed, clamps on), including the parachute q / design q.

## The owner's look in the game (2026-10-29)

> First off, the live menu for the pod is bloated, those components should not be listed, just the part and aggregate stats. The throttle closed shut off worked, but I was not able to run the first stage action group again to turn the engines back on. Plus the plumes still don't totally mesh with their nozzles cleanly, there is a gap/thickness disrepancy. […] Crew limits: rough physiology+NASA data is fine, just get something consistent (no instant deaths, statistical and time integrated for radiation and g's. No injuries, the crew members just die past a threshold. Everything else may be your judgement

- **Pod card:** the live card of the pod lists its components; it should show only the part and its aggregate stats. **Fixed:** what a part's components carry is summed by kind on its card (`part_card::inside`): engines (states, total thrust, ignitions), tanks (each fluid's total, the highest pressure), bottles, batteries, RCS blocks, valves and mechanisms. A part's own items keep their sections (a Merlin's engine, the booster tank's two compartments), and its components' commands are left to the systems screen.
- **AGAIN:** closing the throttle shut the engines down (works), but running the first stage again did not relight them. Cause: only the active stage had AGAIN, and once Liftoff ran, Ignition was no longer on the bar. **Fixed:** every stage already run is listed under the active one with its AGAIN (`Place::Done`). Tests: the sim relights all nine on the pad after a throttle shutdown, and off the pad only E1, E5 and E9.
- **Nozzle and plume:** still a gap, and the plume's thickness at the exit does not match the bell's exit. **Cause:** the soot's radial profile was Simons' `cos^(2/(γ−1))` (exponent ≈ 10) right across the exit. That put the soot at half density by a quarter of the radius, so the jet was drawn a third as wide as the bell, with a soft edge that read as a gap. **Fixed:** the soot is even inside a core that starts at the exit's radius and shrinks to the axis over the potential core, with Simons' profile outside it (`exhaust::profile`, mirrored in `exhaust.wgsl`; tests). Seen on screen: the MVac's plume now leaves the lip at the bell's width (demo at `SUNSCATTER_DEMO_SIZE=3024x1964 SUNSCATTER_DEMO_FAR=0.25`, shot 16). The booster's engines are behind the action bar in every demo shot, so they have not been seen.
- **Still grey (open):** at 88 km the MVac's plume is a dark grey smudge against the sunlit Earth, the same shot. Its soot absorbs more than it emits at the 1,330 K exit temperature, and the soot's extinction figures are marked "not checked" in `features/exhaust.md`. The numbers (seed, full flow):

| | Exit gas | Soot | Optical depth across the exit | Afterburning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Merlin 1D, sea level | 1,334 K | 0.5 % | 3.5 | 0.96 |
| MVac, vacuum | 779 K | 0.5 % | 0.9 | 0 |

At ε 165 the MVac's gas leaves at ~780 K, too cold to glow in the visible, and the soot fraction (`propellants.ron`: U, "no published fraction found") makes it nearly opaque. So it only darkens what is behind it. Options for the owner: (a) the real MVac's visible orange is its radiatively cooled niobium extension at ~1,200–1,500 K, so draw the bell's glow from the part's modelled temperature; (b) a lower soot fraction for the main flow (the sooty turbine exhaust is a film along the extension), which leaves the plume faint and nearly invisible in vacuum; (c) a propellant colour marked (E) for a plume too cold to glow. The lead's recommendation is (a) + (b): physical, and faint rather than grey.
- **Crew limits (decided):** rough physiology with NASA data (NASA-STD-3001 where it has a number), one consistent set. No instant deaths: radiation and g are time-integrated doses with a statistical (probabilistic) outcome. No injuries: a crew member is alive until past a threshold, then dies. Written as **D116**. Not built yet: `systems::crew` still records injuries over NASA-STD-3001's limits; the doses replace them.
- **The other open questions:** the lead's judgement (logical defaults, recorded here).
