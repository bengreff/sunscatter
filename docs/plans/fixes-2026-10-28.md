## The owner's answers (2026-10-29)

- **Plume at altitude:** "The engine plume SHOULD be faint, but it just seems odd that it is such a dull grey color. Make an assumption based on fuel type if unsure." Faint is right; grey is not. The colour comes from the propellant: kerolox glows orange-yellow from hot soot (its emission, not grey scattering), hydrolox is near-invisible with a faint blue-violet, hypergolics a pale orange-pink. Where the data is missing, take the fuel type's value and mark it (E).
- **Flickers:** "The flickers happen when landed on the launchpad." Reproduce on the pad (Falcon 9, landed, clamps on), including the parachute q / design q.

## The owner's look in the game (2026-10-29)

> First off, the live menu for the pod is bloated, those components should not be listed, just the part and aggregate stats. The throttle closed shut off worked, but I was not able to run the first stage action group again to turn the engines back on. Plus the plumes still don't totally mesh with their nozzles cleanly, there is a gap/thickness disrepancy. - **Pod card:** the live card of the pod lists its components; it should show only the part and its aggregate stats. **Fixed:** what a part's components carry is summed by kind on its card (`part_card::inside`): engines (states, total thrust, ignitions), tanks (each fluid's total, the highest pressure), bottles, batteries, RCS blocks, valves and mechanisms. A part's own items keep their sections (a Merlin's engine, the booster tank's two compartments), and its components' commands are left to the systems screen.
- **AGAIN:** closing the throttle shut the engines down (works), but running the first stage again did not relight them. Cause: only the active stage had AGAIN, and once Liftoff ran, Ignition was no longer on the bar. **Fixed:** every stage already run is listed under the active one with its AGAIN (`Place::Done`). Tests: the sim relights all nine on the pad after a throttle shutdown, and off the pad only E1, E5 and E9.
- **Nozzle and plume:** still a gap, and the plume's thickness at the exit does not match the bell's exit. The plume-start tests pass, so compare against the drawn bell's exit radius, not the sim's.
- **Crew limits (decided):** rough physiology with NASA data (NASA-STD-3001 where it has a number), one consistent set. No instant deaths: radiation and g are time-integrated doses with a statistical (probabilistic) outcome. No injuries: a crew member is alive until past a threshold, then dies. Written as **D116**. Not built yet: `systems::crew` still records injuries over NASA-STD-3001's limits; the doses replace them.
- **The other open questions:** the lead's judgement (logical defaults, recorded here).
