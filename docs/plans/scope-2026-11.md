# Scope reassessment, November 2026 (draft for the design sessions)

Written by the Director from the owner interview of 2026-11-02/03 ([interview](../archive/interviews/interview-2026-11-02-scope.md), 68 answers). Status: **draft**. Vision, pillars, scope, look and feel, and gameplay balance are the owner's calls (answer 68). Everything marked *proposal* is the Director's and gets settled in the design sessions.

## 1. The diagnosis

The owner's concern: "we are overengineering meaningless specifics rather than building a versatile foundation for a generalized simulator."

What the repo shows (2026-11-02, 122k lines of Rust):
- **The goal pulled toward specifics.** v0.2 was "Crew Dragon to a station and back, the booster lands" (D117, D123). Every brief then asked what *that* flight needs: Dragon's trunk joint, its helium bottles, its chute arming rule, its warp-stop thresholds. Each answer was correct, and each one was specific to one vehicle.
- **No written data model.** D100 to D102, D118, D121 and D125 say what a part should be, but no document defines the part and component types, the ports, the parameters, or what is derived and what is stored. Without that, each agent solved its own case. The ~50 owner questions of 30-31 Oct are the symptom: most of them are edge-case rules that a general model would have produced as a result (answer 5).
- **The interface ran ahead of the model.** 12+ agent rounds and 81 interface findings were spent on screens for a model that isn't settled yet (answers 5, 44).
- **Swarms.** About 35 agents over 26-31 Oct, many with 500k+ context. Each one patched its own corner, which is how specifics pile up.

What is sound and stays (answer 63): ephemeris, N-body and frames; determinism and the warp-invariance suites; MCP and headless mission tests; the knowledge/sensor model (D106). Bevy stays and `sim` stays engine-free (64).

## 2. What sunscatter is (owner's answers, condensed)

- **A professional-grade tool and a game for "crazed space nerds", with equal weight** (2). It should be credible for trajectory and Δv design, vehicle sizing and trades, subsystem behaviour over a mission, and ascent/entry/landing flight dynamics (1). Not credible for exact hardware or assembly specifics.
- **Generality is the goal, and calibration comes first** (3, 7). Every part is an instance of a generic type with scalable parameters. Real vehicles are configurations of those parts, plus scripts and a few hand-set calibration values. Each domain has one model: one data and operating system, one thermal/aero model. The exception is the engine *interior*: its performance comes from tables built from inquiry/CRUCIBLE or real data (9).
- **Fidelity by consequence** (6). Each quantity is modelled at the level where its error stops changing mission outcomes.
- **Scope:** 100 ly first, growing to 100-300 ly (23); at least **10,000 years** (21; the vision says 1,000); warp up to **100,000-500,000x** (26; the vision says 10⁶). v1 is present-day technology. The foundation must later express NTR/NEP/SEP, fusion, antimatter, beamed power and sails, plasma radiators and particle shields, but nothing that needs unknown physics (29).
- **Game:** design > simulate > iterate, and live flying, come first. Organization, infrastructure, interplanetary trade, physics-based strategy and true discovery are "still SUPER important" (33). One company; the ISS is gone; other organizations are scripted and mostly offer contracts (35). Crew are minimal: mass, consumables and dose limits (36). There is an AI interface alongside the UI, with strict parity (33, 39).

## 3. The foundation: architecture proposal

### 3.1 Craft data model (design session 1)
*Proposal*, from answers 9-16:
- **Assembly tree, recursive.** Craft > assemblies > parts > components, to whatever depth the hardware has. Example: Electron > Stage 1 > Rutherford x9 > {chamber+nozzle (table), e-pump x2, main valves, gimbal actuators, battery feed}.
- **System graphs across the tree.** Power, fluid, data, thermal and structural load paths. Each component is a node in the tree and in one or more graphs. Graphs cross part boundaries only through **ports** on mount faces: fluid couplings, connectors, umbilicals (16). Joining parts joins their ports.
- **Types + parameters + calibration.** One model per type (`tank.cylindrical`, `panel.solar`, `valve.ball`, `line.tube`, `battery.cell_pack`...). Parameters scale it (radius, length, wall material, cell count). Data trust: **derived > published > estimate** (11). Mass comes from geometry and material, and a published figure *calibrates* the derived one. A mismatch is logged as a model error, never silently overridden. This reverses D115.
- **Routing.** Wiring and plumbing are auto-routed inside a part's volume, around the components that block them, with bend radius, clearance and bundling. Route length and bends set mass, pressure drop, resistance and heat leak (15). The player can pin waypoints.
- **Engine families** have their own interfaces (30). A chemical engine's components (pumps, valves, lines, gimbals, igniters) are simulated like any other. Its chamber and nozzle are defined geometry. What happens inside comes from a table: thrust, Isp, mixture, nozzle heat, plume. CRUCIBLE tables come first; an in-game designer that calls CRUCIBLE comes later (31).
- **The first pass covers** structure, tanks and feed; engines; power, avionics and data; crew, thermal and entry (12).
- **Calibration order** (7): Electron or Falcon 1 (small and well documented), then Falcon 9 + Dragon rebuilt from the same types, then a probe (New Horizons or DART, 49). The test of generality: put Dragon on another rocket with an adapter, change the engine count, stretch a tank, all with no code changes.

### 3.2 Domains: one lumped network solver each
*Proposal* (27, 28): fluids, power, thermal and data are each a node/link network (EcosimPro / SINDA / Modelica style) solved for any topology. Each element is modelled at the fidelity its consequences need. Structure: rigid-body motion plus a load path and margin for every joint and part, with failure when a margin goes negative; flex modes come later, only where they matter, such as POGO or long stacks (45). Aero/thermal: a runtime cell/panel model plus boundary layer, calibrated by offline CFD tables per shape class (SU2) and validated on published entry data (48).

### 3.3 Time, warp and fidelity tiers (the hardest problem)
The owner's hard requirement (26): "What cannot happen is a craft's temp is stable at 100x warp, then at 1000x warp it overheats. Unacceptable in any scenario."

Three tiers (41): **physics warp** (atmosphere, near another craft), **coast warp**, and **inactive-craft coast warp**. Every craft really runs its script and its physics; there are **no outcome distributions** (40). A mission whose route depends only on celestial bodies can be **computed ahead independently**, in parallel or offline. A mission that touches a station or another craft can't be (47).

*Proposal*: the rules that make warp invariance true by construction (these are the research spike's hypotheses):
1. **Warp is never an input to the physics.** The warp setting only decides how many canonical steps run per frame. Every step size, every subsystem rate and every event time comes from the state and a fixed epoch grid, never from warp. If that holds, 100x and 1000x run the identical sequence of steps, and their results are bit-identical. The only cost of warp is compute.
2. **Make each canonical step cheap instead of making steps longer.** Linear or near-linear subsystems (thermal networks, batteries, tank blowdown) use exponential or analytic integrators, which are exact over a step at any length for piecewise-constant inputs. Then a long step and many short steps agree to round-off, and the scheduler can merge steps on a fixed power-of-two grid.
3. **Quiescence certificates.** A subsystem at a provable steady state, or on an analytic trajectory (a coast, a constant-current discharge), is advanced in closed form until the next event, with the error bound recorded. This is how one 5,000-part craft could reach 100,000x (47).
4. **Events located exactly.** Valve limits, thresholds and contact are found by root-finding on the canonical grid, not detected late at frame boundaries.
5. **Seeded randomness by (entity, epoch).** Counter-based RNG (Philox-style) keyed on the entity and the epoch bin, so a draw never depends on step order or warp. Note that every failure still needs a modelled cause (46). Randomness only seeds variation, such as a manufacturing flaw's size.

The trade-offs, with worked examples, are the job of the warp spike (answer 26 asks for "precise examples with trade-offs in terms of reproducibility"): a radiator panel during eclipse entry, a tank's boil-off over a 9-month coast, a battery near a load-shed threshold, and a chute deploying at a pressure trigger during entry, each run at 1x / 10³x / 10⁵x.

### 3.4 Adopt instead of build
License: open source, so GPL is fine (17). Runtime: Rust, with C/C++ over FFI where it's deterministic; Python and Fortran only offline (18).

| Domain | Candidate | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluid properties | CoolProp (MIT, C++) | runtime real-gas properties for propellants, pressurants, coolants | spike: FFI build, determinism check |
| Climate, any planet | ExoPlaSim (GPL, built for exoplanets), Isca (idealised GCM framework), SpeedyWeather.jl (fast, MIT) | offline per-world climatology (seasonal and diurnal wind, temperature, density, cloud); runtime weather = climatology + seeded systems (21, D113); re-runnable as orbits and climates change over centuries | spike: one Mars and one Titan climatology |
| Upper atmosphere | NRLMSIS 2.1 | Earth's thermosphere vs solar activity | literature |
| Spacecraft dynamics, GNC, subsystems | Basilisk (ISC, flight-proven), NASA 42 | reference architecture and validation oracle; possibly FFI modules | literature + one oracle comparison |
| Rigid body, contact | Rapier (Rust, cross-platform determinism mode) | contact, joints, breakup, debris | spike: bit-identical Mac vs Windows |
| Trajectories | GMAT, Orekit | oracles for propagation, maneuvers, transfers | oracle tests |
| Ascent/entry | JSBSim, OpenRocket, published reconstructions | oracles | oracle tests |
| Engines | CEA, CRUCIBLE | engine tables; CRUCIBLE goes beyond CEA (fusion, plasma, nozzle shape) | tables first |
| Aero tables | SU2 (offline CFD) | per-shape-class calibration | later |
| Debris environment | ESA MASTER / NASA ORDEM-style flux | collision risk from the general debris population (47) | literature |
| Exoplanets | NASA Exoplanet Archive, Gaia | real catalog where verified (23) | literature |

The owner's caveat (20): integrated systems spanning many regimes can't be validated against any one tool, only their pieces. So the oracles validate domains, and the integrated behaviour is checked by consistency and mission tests.

### 3.5 Performance targets (replaces D081 and D121's numbers)
- Live play: about 10 controlled craft plus hundreds of short-lived debris pieces. Debris is deleted quickly; collision risk from old debris comes from the flux model, not from tracked pieces (47).
- Heavy warp: **100,000x effective with those 10 craft.**
- The active craft: 5,000 parts. An offline run of a 5,000-part craft with all its interacting systems reaches 100,000x.
- Extreme case: a 5,000-part craft burning up and breaking into hundreds of debris pieces stays at **10 fps or better** (49). Otherwise 60 fps on the M2 Pro at lowest graphics.

## 4. The part view (owner's spec, answer 14)
Click a part in ship view: it isolates and zooms in, shows its internals, and a side panel opens with the whole part's data. From there you can:
- click a component in the 3D view, or in the panel, to isolate *that* component (recursively);
- pull up graphs of any quantity;
- switch viewing layers at the top: structure, fluids, power, data, thermal (cell-by-cell temperatures), loads;
- toggle between the exterior view and the internal component view.

Style: engineering-tool clean, colour-coded layers, crisp labels. Each part also has one realistic-lite exterior, used in flight and as one layer in ship view (42). Routes are drawn as routed. The existing interface is frozen until this is designed (44).

## 5. The game layer (proposals to settle in later sessions)
- **Industry:** process chains with short recipes ("structural metal sheet", "insulation", "pipe", not "tank wall"); **one inventory per planet/body**; goods move between bodies only by real flights; in-space infrastructure (depots, tethers, mass drivers, beamed power) is physical (57-61). Stations and colonies use the ship component model without structure or placement (58).
- **Science:** physical instruments make data products that must be downlinked; advancement is abstracted into thousands of defined data fields and milestones with fixed, logical rewards (54). Each craft and colony carries its own record of known tech and milestones, and results travel at light speed.
- **Science categories** (*proposal*, 14; the owner asked the Director to propose 5-20). "World" means it advances slowly without the player:

| Category | Advances with | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Propulsion | R&D, flight tests | engine restarts, new cycles, electric propulsion hours |
| Structures and materials | world + R&D | composites, thermal protection, in-space welding |
| Power and energy | world + R&D | cells, batteries, fission and fusion power |
| Thermal control | R&D | radiators, cryogenic storage, boil-off |
| Computing and avionics | world (fast) + R&D | rad-hard chips, autonomy |
| Communications | world + R&D | optical links, deep-space networks |
| Guidance and navigation | R&D, flight data | precision landing, autonomous docking |
| Manufacturing | world + R&D, off-world | first chip in space, regolith processing |
| Life sciences and medicine | world + flight data | 5 years in zero-g, interplanetary biology |
| Planetary science | missions only | Titan's density curve, interiors, atmospheres |
| Astrophysics and fundamental physics | missions only | black hole observations, ISM, relativity tests |
| Nuclear and plasma physics | R&D, tests in space | ICF pulse characteristics, magnetic nozzles |
| Astrobiology | missions only | extrasolar life, one milestone per data field |

- **Tech progression:** present day at the start; readiness grows by funding, prototypes and test campaigns, inside a physics-gated tree; there's a sandbox mode (32).
- **Worlds:** the real catalog where verified, unknowns filled in, and the rest generated statistically to match all observations. Major systems are hand-built from a consistent set of formation and life assumptions (LLM-assisted), and only the "boring" bodies are procedural. A showcase alien-program system sits elsewhere: the owner's Tartarus-Loom (`~/three-body-islands/system/catalog_full.md`) or Calder (`~/calder-system`) (23). The interstellar physics carried from day one: special relativity, stellar motions, the interstellar medium, the galactic tide (62).
- **Modding:** data-first, open formats. Editing inside in-universe ranges is free; going outside them makes a declared mod that is saved separately, so saves and craft can tell modded from unmodded. Every intended action has an in-game interface (56).

## 6. Redefined v0.2 (answer 49)
v0.2 is complete when:
1. Electron or Falcon 1 is built only from generic part types and parameters, flies a published ascent to orbit within tolerances, and its routed internals show in the part view.
2. Falcon 9 + Dragon are rebuilt on the same types, plus the composability test (Dragon on another booster, engine count changed, tank stretched).
3. One probe (New Horizons or DART) flies its mission profile.
4. The warp and scale benchmarks pass: 10 craft at 100,000x; a 5,000-part offline run at 100,000x; outcomes warp-invariant (the section 3.3 tests); the 5,000-part breakup at 10 fps or better.
5. The ship software those vehicles need ships with them, kept minimal: the pilot flies, with an optional fuller debug autopilot (5).
