# Feature: the systems screen and the action bar

**Status:** *playable* (2026-10-28, SYSTEMS's redesign; the thermal mesh and part isolation 2026-10-31, INTERFACE, from the owner's request: "thermal actually shows the grid level thermal mesh, plus clicking on a part isolates it and zooms in while pressing esc or closing that window goes back to the original layer view of the part"; built, table-tested and seen in the demo's shots of the Falcon 9 on the pad at 1600×900 and 3024×1964, not yet by the owner). Decisions D100, D104, D105, D106, D107, D108, D111; the design is [ship-model.md](ship-model.md) §7–§8; the owner's words are in [interview-2026-10-26-parts.md](../archive/interviews/interview-2026-10-26-parts.md) (final question 2) and [interview-2026-10-27-flight-ui.md](../archive/interviews/interview-2026-10-27-flight-ui.md) (answers 36–37: layers first, structure / fluids / electrical; "maybe be a bit more clear and organized about part stats. Pop up menus welcome."). The Falcon 9 flies in the game ([parts.md](parts.md)).

The owner: "Systems should be an entire new screen separate from flight. Flight has essential info, but to truly control every bit of the craft you need a whole separate screen. Here the primary visualization is of your craft and can switch between layers, like physical, structural, thermal, electrical, and more. Plus all the features you mentioned. The idea is you can define stages and save functions to a mini-systems panel shortcut that you can activate during flight."

## Prior work

| Source | Mechanism | Taken | Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| KSP | The VAB's staging stack and action groups (1–0, custom groups), the part action window | Stages as an ordered list run by Space; functions as named command lists with keys; a part's own commands where it is selected | Action groups only in the editor; keys that shadow flight keys |
| KSP's tracking station | A screen of its own, the flight paused from view | A full screen over the flight view, the clock running | — |
| Real mission control displays (SpaceX's Dragon, the Shuttle's systems displays) | One display per system: power, propulsion, life support, each with the vehicle schematic | Layers on one view of the craft | Schematics drawn by hand: the layers colour the craft's own parts |

## The screen (`game::systems_screen`)

- **Ways in:** **Tab** opens and closes it; **Esc** backs out one level (`pause::on_escape`): an isolated part back to the layer view, then the screen closed (before the pause menu). The action bar's SYSTEMS button, the menus' links ("The systems screen: DATA") and the part cards' "more ›" open it through one entry point, `SystemsScreen::open_at(layer, part)`: at the layer given, and with a part that part selected; a part without a layer shows the part's main layer (`Layer::main`: tanks Plumbing, a battery Electrical, a computer Data, else Structural). While it is open the clock runs, warp keys act, flight keys do not (`InputContext::Systems`).
- **The view** is the active craft alone: its own camera renders render layer 1 into an image the screen shows; no planets, a dark background, one light. Every part is drawn alone from its own mesh (the flight view's, `ShipModels`), parts on mechanisms turned from the ship state. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, click to select (a ray against each part's triangles, `rules::pick`).
- **Layers** (`rules::Layer`, `rules::colour`, from `rules::facts`; each layer's colours explained under the layer bar, `Layer::note`):
  - *Physical*: the flight view's colours (finish or material albedo; the pad's mount concrete grey).
  - *Structural*: each part by the joint to its parent: its load over its ultimate (`JointRun::ratio`) green to red where a strain gauge measures it (debug mode: everywhere), else its kind: integral white, bolted grey, separation amber, hold-down concrete; separated ghosted.
  - *Thermal*: the thermal network itself (`systems_screen::thermal`). Each skin cell is drawn on the simulation's own surface (`Craft::surface`, `Cells::tri_cell`) in its own temperature, blue at 100 K through green and yellow to red at its limit, with the cells' boundaries as a grid; the interior nodes (the volume grid's cubes of `node_size`, each as big as its inside share, at its samples' centroid) are drawn as cubes behind a **cut-away** that faces the camera, moved by the layer bar's slider (none to all; half by default). D106: a part a temperature sensor measures is coloured; the rest show the grid in a neutral "not measured" grey; debug mode colours everything. Cost: the meshes are built once per configuration, per part (so mechanisms turn them and isolation hides them), and read one texture of a texel per cell and node, so a frame rewrites only the texture.
  - *Plumbing*: tanks by their fluid (LOX pale blue, RP-1 amber, MMH yellow, NTO rust), brighter the fuller where a level sensor measures it (else a middle shade); parts with bottles, valves, engines or thrusters grey; the rest ghosted.
  - *Electrical*: sources (batteries) green, parts with loads orange, bus members yellow.
  - *Data*: computers cyan, sensors violet, linked actuators blue.
  - *Health*: integrity and damage (heat past a part's rating, vibration past its qualification), green to red.
- **Networks** (`rules::networks`): the plumbing, electrical and data layers also draw their networks as lines over the parts (gizmos on the view's layer): each plumbing line from the owner of one port to the other's, each bus member to the bus's first, each computer to what it commands (a component at its place, a part at its centre).
- **The left column** switches between SYSTEM-WIDE (the default) and PARTS (`SystemsScreen::left`, owner 2026-10-28: "that menu toggles between system wide and parts list with a default of system wide").
- **The layer as a whole** (left column, SYSTEM-WIDE; `summary::summary`, pure): before any part, what the layer says about the craft. *Physical*: parts, components, mass, dry mass. *Structural*: mass, dry mass (the mass now to the precision the tank sensors give and the dry mass, the pad's mount left out: `sim::systems::estimate::aboard`, the staging bar's own rule), centre of mass above the lowest point aboard (`sim::systems::estimate::centre`: the same parts aboard, to the precision the tank sensors give, through `format::metres`), the g-limit against the load now (`sim::systems::g_limit`) and what sets it, the joints nearest their ultimate. *Thermal*: the parts nearest their limits. *Plumbing*: each tank's contents against its capacity (`format::amount`, the card's and the staging bar's rule), every tank's and bottle's pressure against its burst (`format::pressure`, the card's rule: bar, at most two decimals under 10 bar, one under 100, none above, to the reading's precision; the limits by `format::bar`), what each line's owner feeds, each burning engine's flow (`propulsion::steady`) and the total. *Electrical*: the Power menu's own sections (`menus::power`: buses with volts, load, solar and the pad's feed; batteries; what is without power and why). *Data*: the computers and what they command, the sensors by kind, how many of each measurable quantity the craft measures. *Health*: how many parts are sound, the most worn, what is lost and why. Rows about a part or component are links: a click selects it.
- **Rows against a limit** read `value / limit` and are coloured by the one rule (`format::limit_level`, `theme::level_colour`: amber from 80 %, red at it), the same as the navball's G row and the part cards. A term's hover explains it (`inspect::term`: ullage pressure, joint load, axial capacity, Isp, integrity, the damages, the g-limit…).
- **The part list** (left column, PARTS): the attachment tree, each part's components under it; a click selects it.
- **Isolation** (owner, 2026-10-31): a click on a part (in the view, the part list or a row about it; a component isolates its part) isolates it: the other parts are ghosted and the camera eases to frame it (`rules::frame`, `rules::ease`), keeping its angle. While one is isolated a click picks it first, through its ghosts; a click on a ghost isolates that part instead (`rules::pick_isolating`); a click on nothing changes nothing. **Esc** or the right column's **×** puts back the layer view as it was before the first isolation: its layer, camera pose, left column and selection (`SystemsScreen::back`); a second Esc closes the screen. Opened at a part from flight (a card's "more ›", a menu's link) the part is isolated on its main layer, and going back stays on that layer. Closing the screen puts the layer view back, so it opens on it again. The camera frames the whole craft (or part) in the view's narrower direction (it cut the Falcon 9's nose before).
- **The selection** (right column; no pop-ups, owner 2026-10-28: "Clicking on a part in the systems menu should NOT open a pop up menu, instead all the info for that part should be displayed in the panel on the right"): the part or component selected, × clears it, its commands on top (`inspect::commands`: ignite or shut down its engines, deploy or retract its mechanism, open or close its valve, its thrusters on or off, a component's power, its joint's separation or the clamps' release; each a `GameCommand::Ship`), then everything about it (`inspect::details`) in **one layout for every part**: PART (type, material, finish), STRUCTURE (dry, derived and unmodelled mass, its joint, the joint's load / 100 %, allowable line load, axial capacity; dashes where the craft gives the joint no capacity, `sim::systems::structure::rated`), then a group per function (ENGINE: status, chamber pressure, ignitions left (`inspect::ignitions_left`, the part card's rule too: unlimited for a hypergolic engine, the pad's supply said while clamped), own throttle, selection, thrust and Isp at vacuum and sea level, throttle range, gimbal, propellants, igniter; TANK: liquid / capacity, ullage pressure / burst, operating pressure, temperatures; MECHANISM; AERO SURFACE: deflection now / its range), THERMAL (skin and interior / their limits), CONDITION (integrity, thermal and vibration damage / 1, run time, starts or cycles against its rated life, the interior's peak temperature / its rating), RATINGS, FAILURE MODES, COMPONENTS (each a link). A component: its type, its part (a link), mass, power, then its bottle (contents of its full load through its pressure transducer, `inspect::bottle_contents` and `format::amount`, the RCS menu's feeds' rule; pressure / burst), battery (charge of its capacity with its share, `format::charge`: the Power menu's and the part card's rule; the bus it feeds now), valve (cycles / rated), thrusters, engines, sensor, computer, seat.
- **The editors** (bottom): the stage sequence (Space runs the next) and the saved functions, as drafts: add, remove, rename, reorder an entry; add a command from every command the craft takes (`edit::catalogue`: engine groups and owners, mechanisms one by one and a pattern together `leg#*`, valves, separations, the clamps, the parachutes, thruster blocks) and remove or reorder commands; a function's key, its pin and a run button. The stages already run are shown but locked, tagged RUN (D114, D111: the player cannot draft a change that would be refused; `edit::allowed`; AGAIN on the staging bar runs one again). **Save to the vessel** sends the whole list (`GameCommand::SetStages`/`SetFunctions` → `Vessel::set_stages`/`set_functions`, every command checked against the craft or carried from the program, refused whole with the reasons in a toast); it is the vessel's state (D107), saved with it. A list not being edited follows the vessel's; after a separation each piece keeps the whole program and the editors show its view (`sim::craft::program`: the entries and commands acting on that piece), an entry moving past its neighbours in the view (`edit::moves`) while the other pieces' stay where they are; "not saved yet" while a draft differs.

- **The programs** (bottom, the third tab, PROGRAMS; D126: "the autopilot is a switch with a config menu", D128 layer 2; `systems_screen::programs`): every program on the ship computer, one row each: the planned burns (`maneuver`), the parachutes' deploy (`chutes`, on a craft with canopies) and each installed program (a load manager: its loads and lines). Each row has its switch (AUTO: it runs by itself; ON PRESS: it runs when pressed for, one run a press; OFF: it does not run, the controls stay the pilot's), a press button where a press does something now (greyed, with why on hover, where not: "it runs by itself", "no burn planned", "nothing left to open"), what it is doing now in its tone ("burn 1 wakes in 12m 04s: waits for a press", "armed: the mains open below 1.80 km", "no working flight computer: it misses its epochs") and what it does (its configuration: the heights, the loads and lines). A switch or a press is `GameCommand::SetSwitch` / `Press` (`programs::set_switch`, `press`: the one rule the menus, the P key and MCP use), sent like any command. Ways in: the Maneuver menu's link (`SystemsScreen::open_tab`), its PROGRAM, Landing ▸ PARACHUTE's DEPLOY; MCP `read {programs: true}`, `set_switch`, `press`, `shoot {screen: systems, tab: programs}`.

**Commands travel** (D067, `commands::route`): from aboard a crewed craft they act at once; to an uncrewed one (the booster after separation, flown from mission control) after the signal's light delay, and not at all without a signal (a toast says so). The same holds for Space on a craft of parts (`GameCommand::Stage(vessel)`) and for saving its stages and functions.

**Readouts through knowledge** (D106; `sim::systems::data`, `game::format::Known`): every value the craft measures is shown to the digits its precision gives, everything else as a dash, and the summaries count what nothing measures ("32 joints (no strain gauge)"); a row's limit colour only where it is measured. The Falcon 9 carries no strain gauges and Dragon's tanks no level sensors, so its joint loads read as dashes outside debug mode, and its mass to the tonne and its centre of mass to the metre or so (each of Dragon's tanks anywhere from empty to full; interface-map finding 41). Debug mode knows everything.

## The action bar (`game::interface::action_bar`)

At the top right, the staging bar's width, the staging bar below it (D080, D111; owner, 2026-10-28): SYSTEMS (Tab), then each function pinned in the editors as a button, run by a click, wrapping. Nothing for the test craft.

## Tests

Rules with table tests: the thermal mesh (every cell's triangles once in its part reading its texel, the cells' boundaries, every node a cube in its part, the cut, the texels measured and not), isolation and going back (a sequence of selections, orbits, layer changes, Esc and ×), the framing, the easing and the isolating pick; facts per part, every layer's colours and networks, the ramps, the tree, the camera's pose, the pick, the regions, each part's main layer; the summaries (every layer on the test rocket, blind and all-knowing; the hottest and the most worn first, coloured; electrical as the Power menu's); the inspector's one layout, groups and commands for a tank, an engine, a leg and components, dashes without a sensor; `open_at` (layer, part, the main layer once the facts are known) and one selection, the left column starting SYSTEM-WIDE; the details keep to the right column's width (an egui frame test); the editors' catalogue and edits, and the stages already run locked; drafts following the vessel; the programs' rows (the planned burns by switch and step, the parachutes by switch, state and altitude reading, an installed program by switch and computer, the one rule for a switch and a press, the Falcon 9's defaults) and the programs' tab opened from the Maneuver menu; `format::limit_level`; the part card's temperature colour.
