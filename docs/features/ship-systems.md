# Feature: ship systems

**Status:** the systems of a craft of catalog parts (D103–D106; `docs/design/ship-model.md` §5–§6), 2026-10-27. RUNTIME: commands, the pad's clamps, plumbing, propulsion, mechanisms, attitude control, the knowledge API (its sections). SYSTEMS2: structure, failures, power, crew (below). Each is **playable** in `sim` for the Falcon 9 Crew (tests below), drawn by the systems screen where noted; what is left is listed per section.

## Commands, the sequence and the pad (`sim::systems::{command, runtime}`, wired by `sim::vessel::ship_tick`)

**Status: playable.**

- **Orders at epochs** (`Vessel::order_at(at, OrderKind::{Command, Stage, Function})`): applied at the first live tick at or after the epoch, exactly at it while coasting or standing (the vessel stops there), so the same orders give the same flight in any frames (tested at 60 fps against one jump). Light delay (D067) is the sender's: mission control's orders are queued at now + the light time. `Vessel::command`/`next_stage` still apply at the vessel's time. Orders waiting at a split stay with the vessel's own piece.
- **Commands as the systems carry them out:** an ignition starts the engine's start sequence (one of its igniter charges; on the pad, the ground's TEA-TEB, falcon9.md §2.1); on floating propellant it first waits for it to settle (see Propulsion); a shutdown is its tail-off; a deploy moves the mechanism at its actuator's rate (and counts a cycle of its actuator); an engine starts only through a working controller or computer (its own function in, and one linked computer working: not lost, with power).
- **The pad** (falcon9.md §1.2, §4.2): while the hold-down clamps hold the vessel and its systems are busy, it ticks in place fixed to the ground: the Merlins spool up and burn propellant, the clamps carry m·a_proper − F_thrust (2.13 MN at full thrust, 4 % of their capacity; above it they tear out). The sequence's "Liftoff" **arms** the release: the clamps let go at the first tick every lit engine runs at its rated thrust (2.0 s after ignition at full throttle) — with the throttle below 100 % they wait; an engine that fails or stops first aborts the launch (all engines shut down, the clamps held, "Pad abort"). A release commanded by hand (systems screen) lets go at once.
- **The log** (`Vessel::ship_events`): stages, aborts, failures, settling, depletion — for the game's toasts.

## Plumbing (`sim::systems::plumbing`)

**Status: playable** (slosh pendulums not built).

- **Tanks:** pressure = the pressurant's ideal-gas pressure in the ullage + the liquid's vapour pressure; the liquid's density from its table at its temperature. The liquid warms from the air through its wetted wall (9 W/m²K: falcon9.md §3.6's 2 kW/m² on a frosted LOX tank, U) and from the sunlight its wetted wall absorbs; tanks inside components (Dragon's) are held by their heaters. Above the relief pressure (the tank's MEOP) the ullage vents, its vapour with it; at saturation the heat boils off Q/L, vented. Densified LOX warms 0.72 K per 10 min on the pad (DATA ~0.6) and its 2 % ullage vents a little helium as it expands.
- **Pressurisation** (falcon9.md §3.3): each tank's ullage is held at its loading pressure from the bottles plumbed to it, within the lines' choked flow (Cd 0.8, U); heated helium (250 K into LOX, 300 K into RP-1, U) while a pump-fed engine it feeds is lit, else at the bottle's temperature; bottles blow down isentropically and exchange heat with the liquid they sit in (τ 60 s, U); the ullage expands adiabatically, mixes, relaxes towards the liquid (τ 300 s, U). A first-stage burn holds 3.5 bar on 251 kg of helium (DATA ~260 kg). Sets `Runtime::pressure_regulated` (the structure then reads the real pressures). Ullage gas counts in the vessel's mass.
- **Feed:** NPSP = ullage + ρ·a·(liquid height + the line's drop to the inlet) − 0.05 MPa at full flow (U) − vapour pressure: 9.4 bar at the Merlins at liftoff (DATA 10.1).
- **Settling** (falcon9.md §3.5): the liquid's bulk moves along the tank's axis under the proper acceleration at the tank (centre of mass + rotation) while the Bond number exceeds 1, and disperses over the capillary time √(ρR³/σ) below it; the outlet is covered within 5 cm + 10 % of the liquid's height (U, generous). A separation system's push jolts the liquids (the stage pushed forward settles). MECO's coast moves them centimetres; after a long coast they float until settled in √(2x/a).
- **On the lattice** (coasting or standing, 60 s, from where the slow part last stepped): heat, relief, boil-off, the bottles' and ullages' exchange, dispersal. A day on rails: the second stage's densified LOX warms 2.66 K, vents 1.5 kg of helium; at saturation it boils off 1,451 kg/day; the same bits in any frames. A craft of parts' contents after a coast are its ship state's (the segment carries its starting mass: vented gas is ≪ the vessel's).
- **Not done:** slosh pendulums (D103's "limited give"), line transients, valves' effect on flow (prevalves are state only), propellant temperature stratification, the thermal network's wall nodes coupling to the liquids (the liquid takes its own wall's heat directly).

## Propulsion and attitude control (`sim::systems::{propulsion, rcs}`, `sim::vessel::attitude::limits`)

**Status: playable.**

- **One engine model** (falcon9.md §2; replaces `craft::engine::ship_thrust` in flight): off → starting (a smooth step over its start time: 2 s Merlin, 3 s MVac, 0.1 s SuperDraco) → running (within its throttle range: **zero throttle is the minimum, not off** — shut an engine down to stop it) → shutting down (tail-off τ = shutdown impulse / F_vac) → off; or failed. Steady map F = τ·F_vac − p·Aₑ, ṁ = τ·ṁ_full (a Merlin at 40 % at sea level: 296.6 kN). Feeds by mixture ratio from the first tank holding liquid; dry → flameout ("propellant depleted").
- **Inlet trouble** (D105 §6.2): an engine starting on an uncovered outlet ingests gas (start aborted, the charge spent), running it is cut by its controller (restartable); below its NPSP it cavitates likewise. Debug mode turns both off.
- **Settling before a start:** an engine commanded to start on floating propellant waits (≤ 120 s, its ignition kept) while the enabled thrusters that push within 25° of its thrust fire (the second stage's two aft nitrogen nozzles, 400 N), or drag settles it (the booster has none), then lights; **ignite again to start at once** (into floating propellant: gas ingestion). The real timeline's MVac start (8 s after MECO) needs no wait: the pushers and the few seconds of coasting leave it settled.
- **RCS by translation** (`rcs::translate`): the nozzles pushing along a wanted direction (the pilot's `Controls::translate`, body axes, −1..1; an engine's settling) give force and torque from their geometry and draw their gas. Rotation keeps `ShipDef::rcs_authority`. A thruster block fires only with its function in (not lost, powered) and gas in its feed.
- **Gimbal and SAS for a big stack** (craft of parts; the test craft keeps its ideal actuators): the gimbal turns at the running engines' slowest actuator rate (Merlin 20°/s, MVac 10°/s), the realised direction saved (`Runtime::thrust_dir`, the flames follow it); SAS's hold frequency and closing rate come from the angular acceleration *along the error* (a stack's weak roll authority no longer throttles its pitch), each axis saturates alone, rotation is damped at the hold's frequency, slews to far targets (> 3°) are capped at 10°/s up to 1 kPa, ∝ 1/q above, ≥ 0.5°/s, while small errors get full bandwidth. A hand-flown gravity turn (a pitch kick of 4 % gimbal at T+10 s for 2.5 s, then SAS surface prograde) holds α ≤ 0.13° through max-Q and reaches MECO at 83.7 km, 2,346 m/s over the ground, joints ≤ 0.62.
- **Not done:** the engines' heat per engine status (D077 shares stay static), per-engine gimbals (the engines deflect together), RCS rotation by allocation (rotation keeps the per-axis authority; translation is allocated), SuperDraco and Draco tests in flight (they run on the same models: hypergolic, pressure-fed, 0.1 s start).

## Mechanisms and grid fins (`sim::systems::{mechanisms, fins}`, `sim::aero::lattice`, legs in `sim::contact`)

**Status: playable.**

- **Mechanisms** move at their actuators' rates (legs 120° in 3 s, grid fins 90° in 2 s, Dragon's nosecone 100° at 1°/s); the shape (a configuration of its own) changes when they arrive. A mechanism whose actuator is lost or unpowered stays where it is.
- **Lattice fins** (`aero::lattice`): C_N = 1.2·tanh(C_Nα(M)·α/1.2) on the cells' frontal area; C_Nα 4.7, 4.1, 3.9 per radian at Mach 0.5, 1.2, 2.0 (Hiroshima & Tatsumi, ICAS 2004, wind tunnel), the **transonic choking dip** at Mach 0.95 to 65 % (Washington & Miller, AIAA 93-0035; E), ∝ 1/√(M²−1) beyond Mach 2 (E). Their frontal faces' drag stays the bake's Newtonian plate (an overstatement, noted).
- **Grid fins in flight** (`systems::fins`): deployed, each pushes along its tangent at mid-span for the flow's incidence plus its deflection; SAS and the pilot get their authority (least-squares allocation within ±20°, 30°/s); their real forces replace the commanded share, and they reach the joint loads (SYSTEMS2). The booster from 12 km at 300 m/s engines first, upset 5°: it tumbles without them and holds surface retrograde within 0.1° with them.
- **Legs** (`contact`, D066): a foot with a crush core (`Spring::crush`; the Falcon 9's 250 kN per leg, U) pushes at most its plateau while it strokes; a craft of parts' hull contact points leave out a deployed leg's end around its pad (`CellOptions::foot_reach` 0.6 m; the test craft unchanged). The booster lands on one engine from 2 km at 150 m/s (hoverslam, E9 at 75–90 %): touchdown 1.1 m/s, at rest, intact.
- **Not done:** single-use crush cores (they recover), legs' self-levelling, the nosecone's aero while hinged, grid-fin heating and their pump's hydraulic fluid.

## Information (`sim::systems::data`)

**Status: playable (the API); GAME reads it.**

- `Knowledge` (`Vessel::knowledge()`): each `Quantity`'s precision or nothing (a dash): position, velocity, altitude, attitude, angular rate, acceleration; per tank level, pressure and temperature; per bottle pressure; per engine thrust; per battery charge; per joint load; per part temperature; the cabin's pressure, O₂ and CO₂. `Reading { value, precision }` with `decimals()`, `shown()`, `text(unit)`: the truth at its precision, never noise. Debug mode and the test craft know everything exactly.
- From working sensors only (switched on, part intact, not lost or unpowered, linked to a working computer): `inertial` (an IMU: attitude, rates 1e-5 rad/s, acceleration 50 µg), `attitude` (star tracker), `position` (GNSS: position, velocity /30, altitude ×1.5), `rates`, `acceleration`, `velocity`, `altitude`, `tank-level`, `pressure` (tanks), `bottle-pressure`, `temperature`, `strain`, each of its own part; each engine's controller its thrust (1 %); each battery its charge (1 %).
- The Falcon 9 carries falcon9.md §5.2's level sensors, tank and COPV pressure transducers and thermocouples (catalog entries in `data/parts/equipment`); Dragon's propellant gauging is not public (a dash); no strain gauges in flight.
- **Not done:** an uncrewed craft's knowledge delayed to mission control is the game's telemetry delay; GNSS outages (altitude, plasma blackout); dead reckoning from an IMU alone.

## How the Falcon 9 is flown (for the owner)

On the pad: throttle to 100 %, Space ("Ignition": the nine Merlins spool for 2 s against the clamps), Space ("Liftoff": the clamps let go at full thrust; with less throttle they wait). A small pitch kick by hand at ~T+10 s (a degree or two), then SAS on surface prograde for the gravity turn; throttle down to ~75 % around max-Q if you like. Space at MECO (T+~145 s), Space (separation), Space (second stage ignition: the MVac lights; after a long coast its start waits while the nitrogen pods settle it — ignite again to start at once). On the booster (switch to it): the grid fins (mini panel), SAS retrograde (surface), "Booster: three engines" for the entry burn, "Booster: one engine" for the landing burn (E1/E5/E9 have three relights; the others none in flight), the legs (3 s), and cut the engine at touchdown: a single Merlin at its 40 % minimum is more than the empty stage's weight (it cannot hover). Translation (`Controls::translate`) fires the thrusters that push that way.

## Structure (`sim::systems::structure`, gathered by `sim::vessel::ship_loads`)

**Status: playable.** Rigid until it breaks (D103).

- **Loads.** Each live and held tick every intact joint's force and moment, exact for the rigid body: the subtree beyond the joint (its child's side, the capsule being the root) is accelerated by the joint alone, F = Σ mᵢ(a + α×rᵢ + ω×(ω×rᵢ)) − F_ext, the moment about the joint's centre likewise with each part's Iᵢα + ω×Iᵢω (`subtree_loads`, one pass, deepest first). The motion is the one the gathered forces give (`consistent`: a = ΣF/M, α = I⁻¹(τ − ω×Iω)), so a joint carries the same seen from either side. The parts' masses carry their tanks' liquid and ullage gas and their bottles' gas; the pad's mount weighs nothing (ground).
- **Forces** (other than gravity): each engine's thrust at its mount along the direction the tick flew (after the gimbal); the air's hull force and moment (`AeroTick::loads`) split along the slender axis — the forebody's Newtonian pressure on each part's upstream cells, the rest of the drag (friction, base) by wetted area, the normal force by the area facing the crossflow shifted along the axis so its moment is the model's (the totals are the model's exactly; the split is an approximation, the aero model gives no per-part forces); the parachute on its part; the deployed grid fins' forces (`systems::fins`); the ground's normal force at each contact point; the pad's clamps (RUNTIME's held ticks fill the hold-down joint).
- **Check** (`section`, `ratio`): a ring joint's equivalent axial line load N = P/(2πR) ± M/(πR²) in tension against the wall's ultimate (`JointDef::allowable`), in compression against its buckling load where the build gives one (`JointDef::buckling`: a tank's ends sized for an axial load, SP-8007 with its knockdown), shear and torsion flow against the ultimate over √3. A section a tank compartment crosses is relieved by its ullage pressure and liquid head (the gas carries p·πR²) and its buckling raised by SP-8007's pressure gain Δγ; until the plumbing has regulated (`Runtime::pressure_regulated`) the ullage counts at least its loading pressure. Strength falls with temperature as the weaker side's material's ultimate does. No capacity data (an engine mount, a point attachment, an ablator wall, a part on a mechanism — a leg's A-frame and strut, a grid fin's hinge: its feet's overload is the contact's, D066): ratio 0, never breaks; its load is still shown.
- **Snap.** Ratio ≥ 1 (not in debug mode): an alarm ("snapped"/"buckled"), `Effect::Snap(child, load)` → `Breakup::torn`: the child's side tears off with what hangs from it, no separation system fires. Each part's peak ratio goes into its history (`History::peak_load`).
- **Data** (D101): the second stage's axial design load is Dragon and its trunk at the user's guide's 6 g axial limit factor (falcon9.md §6.4), 820 kN; an ablator or core wall carries no stack load (`Wall::allowable` = 0).
- **Falcon 9 numbers** (tests): lift-off: each stack joint carries the mass above × 13.6 m/s², ratios ≤ 0.24; held on the pad at full thrust the octaweb's joint carries the weight above it; max-Q 36 kPa at 4° α with the gimbal balancing: every joint ≤ 0.70 (Dragon ring 0.65); near MECO at 4.9 g the Dragon ring 0.59; the demo's gravity turn (pitch program held directly, ~4° through max-Q): ring peak 0.61; a straight-up full-throttle ascent through MECO, separation and 30 s of MVac: nothing breaks.
- **Cost** (`cost_per_tick`, ignored; release, reference Mac under the night's load): per live tick on the Falcon 9, the joints 0.011 ms, the failures 0.004 ms, power and crew 0.011 ms (ship-model.md §10's budget for the whole systems step: 0.5 ms).
- **Not done:** thruster couples are not among the forces (hundreds of newtons); joints' fatigue (no data); a joint's own stiffness (rigid, D103); a leg's strut capacity (no data: legs are left to the contact).

## Failures (`sim::systems::failure`)

**Status: playable.** D105: from the simulation where it knows, statistics only where appropriate, never when unsure. Debug mode turns all of it off.

- **Fates** (`fates`): each catalog failure mode of each part and component (the build now carries them into the ship definition) draws once E = −ln u, u a counter-based hash (SplitMix64 over FNV-1a) of the save's seed (`SaveGame::seed`, made once per new game; `Vessel::seed_fates`), the launch epoch, the owner and the mode, at the vessel's creation. Its hazard grows per hour of operation (λ·dt), per start or per cycle (−ln(1−p) per event), times its wear beyond its rated life or burn time ((used/rated)²) and 1 + its damage; it fails at H ≥ E. The same flight from the same launch and save fails the same way; a split keeps fates with their parts; warp changes nothing (the hazard is a sum over stored ticks).
- **Outcomes:** benign engine shutdown (`propulsion::stop`, Fail), ignition failure (the start aborted), uncontained engine (destroyed), valve stuck, battery cell lost, function lost (`Runtime::lost`, honoured by RUNTIME's mechanisms and knowledge), part destroyed (`Effect::Destroy` → a wreck of its own, `Destruction::Failed`).
- **Time at temperature** (`damage`): each part's hottest interior node against its rated and destruction temperatures on a Larson–Miller line through its rated hold (1,000 h when not given) and one second at destruction; destroyed at 1. Skin cells are judged against their own materials by `vessel::breakage` (a cell's service temperature is not data); a craft of parts' interiors no longer break at once at their service limit.
- **Vibration** (`damage`): each part's environment — running engines (20 g rms at their mounts by level, ×0.6 per joint), buffeting (5 g rms at 35 kPa, transonic) — against its qualification by Miner's rule (b = 4). An engine fails at 1, a mechanism is lost, other parts carry it as hazard.
- **Bursts and blasts** (`blast`, `physical`): a tank or bottle over its burst pressure bursts with its gas's isentropic energy and its propellants' yield (FAA AC 450.137-1: LOX/RP-1 0 at rest, hypergols 2 %); the overpressure (Kinney–Graham's form of the Kingery–Bulmash data, through the air or a tank's own gas) bursts the tanks and bottles whose ratings it exceeds: cascades (AMOS-6's path is a test).
- **Falcon 9's modes** (falcon9.md §6.3, low ends): Merlin in-flight shutdown 2.2e-3/h and ignition 1.7e-5 per start; MVac restart 8e-5 per start (uncontained); grid-fin pump stall 1.6e-4 per cycle (RUNTIME counts a mechanism's movement as a cycle).
- **Not done:** gas ingestion and cavitation are RUNTIME's engine stops (plumbing); hard starts, pyroshock (no shock ratings), components' own vibration (their environment is their part's, no per-component damage store), fragments; hazards on the rails lattice (no Falcon 9 mode runs there); a blast in vacuum reaches only what is inside a tank.

## Power (`sim::systems::power::step_ship`)

**Status: playable** (numbers in `Runtime::buses`, `Runtime::unpowered`).

- Each live tick and lattice step, every bus: its loads (each component switched on, not lost, at its power) against its sources — the pad's umbilical while the clamps hold (tops the batteries up), its solar cells (body-mounted, facing out from the axis where they sit: Dragon's trunk 8.9 m² at 30 %, 3.6 kW square to the Sun), then its batteries (each to its maximum power, losing I²R in its resistance and warming). Short of power the bus sheds loads, the non-essential first (essential: computers, sensors, valves, thrusters, life support), the largest first; a shed load's function stops (`Runtime::unpowered`) until power returns, with alarms. Surplus charges the batteries. Bus voltage is derived (open-circuit by charge, less the drop).
- **Falcon 9 numbers:** the first stage's bus draws 314 W from its 2 × 2.5 kWh (16 h); Dragon's ~1.2 kW from 4 × 2.5 kWh and its cells.
- **Not done:** the umbilical is cut with the clamps (Dragon's real transfer is T−5 min); battery temperature limits, cells per skin cell, planet albedo.

## Crew and life support (`sim::systems::crew`)

**Status: playable** (the cabin's air in `ShipState::cabin`, injuries in `Runtime::crew`).

- The cabin (its life support's volume, 9.3 m³): O₂, N₂, CO₂, water vapour partial pressures by the ideal-gas law; each crew member per NASA BVAD (0.82 kg O₂ in, 1.04 kg CO₂ and 1.9 kg water out per day, ~100 W). The life support, powered and not lost, removes CO₂ and water to its rates, holds O₂ at 21.3 kPa, the total at 101.3 kPa (N₂) and 295 K; without it the air goes stale (an alarm at 1 kPa of CO₂) and the cabin drifts to the capsule's temperature.
- Acceleration limits (NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2, falcon9.md §6.6): +Ax (body +Z), −Ax and sideways (the more tolerant of ±Ay, +Az: the seats' sideways axes are not data) against the limit for the time each level has been held (windows of levels, log-linear limits); over it, an alarm and an injury recorded.
- **Not done:** Brinkley's dynamic response for landing (its parameters are to be verified, falcon9.md §6.6), suits, deconditioned limits for entry after a long flight, consumables' storage (the life support's O₂ and N₂ are not tanks yet), leaks. The data's CO₂ removal (4.0 kg/day) is a little under four crew's 4.16.
