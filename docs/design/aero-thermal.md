# Aerodynamics, heating and hitbox on surface cells

> Status: **playable** for the aerodynamics, skin heating and the interior volume nodes with engine heat (wired into flight, `sim::vessel::aerothermal`); coolant loops, radiators and heat pipes are later. Decisions: D061 (cells of the model), D062 (rails floor), D064 (debug mode), D065 (skin per cell, clean destruction), D066 (rigid-body contact). Tests are the final specification.

A ship in flight is one 3D model (the test craft's `geometry.ron` for now). Its surface is split into **cells** (`sim::craft`, adaptive to the geometry, ≤ 512 by default). Aerodynamic pressure and friction, heating and cooling all act on those cells; the sums give the force and torque on the rigid body (§3d of the plan) and each cell's skin temperature.

## Data

Per craft (`craft.ron`, the test craft for now):
- per cell (built at load): centroid, outward normal, area, skin areal mass, specific heat, emissivity, neighbours with conductance, contact flag;
- `aero: (cd0)`: the subsonic pressure (form and base) drag coefficient on the projected area (0.8, a blunt body); skin friction is added from the geometry;
- `thermal: (skin_max_k, internal_max_k, node_size, interior_specific_heat, propellant_specific_heat, interior_conductivity, internal_coupling)`: the skin limit and the limit of every interior node; the interior grid's cube edge (1 m); the specific heats of the dry interior (the dry mass less the skin, spread by volume: 900 J/(kg·K)) and of the propellant (2,000); the interior's effective conductivity between nodes (5 W/(m·K)); the conductance from each cell to the node beneath it per unit of the cell's area (2 W/(m²·K));
- `engine.nozzle_heat_fraction` (D077): the share of the jet power ½·F·vₑ absorbed by the radiatively cooled nozzle extension (the geometry's `nozzle` primitive, the bell) while the engine runs: 2.2e-3, ~1 MW at full thrust, so the bell settles at ~1400 K, where coated niobium (C-103) extensions run. Of the 0.5–5 % of the combustion energy that crosses an engine's walls (Sutton & Biblarz, *Rocket Propulsion Elements*), the chamber's share returns to the flow through regenerative cooling and is not a heat source. The bell's cells couple only to the mount's node (it is joined at the throat), at ~1 W/K in all (`coupling: 0.2` W/(m²·K) of bell area; a titanium gimbal block of ~0.01 m² over ~0.1 m is ~0.7 W/K, an engineering estimate: no published value was found). A five-minute full-thrust burn warms the mount's node ~7 K (tested);
- per skin material: `max_k` (its temperature limit; else the craft's `skin_max_k`) and `coupling` (to the node beneath; else `internal_coupling`); a cell is destroyed above its own material's limit, and the flight panel's SKIN MAX shows the cell closest to its limit;
- the effective nose radius per flow direction is derived from the cells (the bake).

Per body (`body.ron`, `sim::body::atmosphere`): the atmosphere as a **table** of (altitude, temperature, pressure, density, mean molar mass), temperature and molar mass linear between rows, pressure and density log-linear; or, for bodies without one, the exponential model (`rho0`, `scale_height`, `p0`, isothermal at T = H·g₀·M/R, λ ∝ 1/ρ from `mean_free_path` at `rho0`). Earth's table is the **U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976** (NOAA-S/T 76-1562, NASA-TM-X-74335) to 1,000 km, 178 rows generated by `tools/us76_table.py`: from the standard's defining equations every 1 km to 86 km (geopotential layers and lapse rates, M/M₀ of its Table 8 above 80 km), its Table I values above, with Braeunig's polynomial fits of Table I between rows where log-linear interpolation would be off by more than 0.5 % (within 0.1 % of the table at every tabulated point). Interpolated, the table is within 0.1 % of the standard at 5, 11, 20, 25, 50, 75, 100, 400 and 1,000 km (tested). Above 86 km the standard is itself a mean-activity model (exospheric temperature 1,000 K), close to NRLMSISE-00 mean conditions; solar-cycle variation is not modelled. The air: `gamma` (1.4), `collision_diameter` for the mean free path λ = kT/(√2·π·d²·p) (US 1976: 3.65e-10 m; 6.63e-8 m at sea level, 0.142 m at 100 km), `sutton_graves_k`; speed of sound √(γRT/M) with the table's M. Flight sees air only below `top` (Earth 150 km): the live boundary and the rails floor (D062); the table above it is read by `Atmosphere::profile` only (orbital decay above 150 km is not modelled). Stars carry a `luminosity` (the Sun: 3.828e26 W). The rails floor (D062, done).

## The bake (once per craft design, deterministic)

`sim::craft::design::CraftDesign` holds the bake and the thermal network, built on first use and shared by every vessel of the design (`Arc`); vessels hold it in a `DesignSlot` that is never saved and is rebuilt from the craft's files after a load (the bake is deterministic: same bits). For the 642 directions **d** of a level-3 geodesic grid in body axes (193 ms for the test craft in release; level 4, 2,562 directions, 644 ms):
1. **Exposure:** the surface triangles facing **d** are rasterised onto a 64×64 z-buffer orthogonal to it, spanning the craft's projection (square pixels: a slender body seen end-on is resolved like one seen broadside; with the bounding sphere's square it lost 23 % of a fineness-10 body's frontal area); a cell's exposure is the fraction of its covered pixels it wins (u8). Cells smaller than a pixel are judged by the pixel under their centroid.
2. **Nose radius** for **d**: a regression of the lateral offsets on the lateral normal components over the stagnation region (exact for a sphere), capped by the projected disc.
3. **Extent** of the craft along **d** (vertex projections), for the fineness along the flow.
4. **Wave drag** along **d** (`aero::wave`): the area distribution A(x) at 65 stations along **d** from the triangles (A(x) = −Σ A_t·(n_t·d) upstream of x; faces turned downstream more than 45° do not close it: the separated wake keeps their area), and von Kármán's slender-body integral in Fourier form, x = (l/2)(1 − cos θ), A′ = Σ aₙ sin nθ, D/q = (π/4)·Σ n·aₙ², truncated at 16 terms (the smoothing; a Sears–Haack body is exact to 1 %, 64 terms overshoot 5 % from discretisation noise).

Once per design, not per direction: each cell's second moment of area (its extent along any direction, for friction run lengths); the **body sections** (`aero::slender::BodySections`): the long axis (principal axis of largest spread of the surface area, by power iteration) and the cross-section area at 65 stations along it from the triangles by the divergence theorem, A(x) = −Σ A_t·(n_t·â) upstream of x; the **fins**: cells whose anti-parallel opposite face is within 3 % of the craft's size (a ray through the part), grouped by primitive, kept when each side is a plane (normals within 8°) and the part is thin for its size (thickness < 0.1·√planform). The test craft has none (legs are round, feet are stubby discs); +30 ms of bake (the ray casts).

Memory: 642 × 512 cells ≈ 321 KB per design. The force sums are **not** baked: they are formed per evaluation over the cells, with the exposure interpolated barycentrically between the three grid directions around the flow and the incidence from the actual flow (~3 µs for 512 cells). Baking the sums was measured 2 % off on a cone at 5° (they are quadratic in the direction). 

Also once per design: the **mean drag area** (the hypersonic continuum drag area averaged over the grid's directions, ≈ 0.92 × the mean projected area), the attitude-independent drag of coasts and predictions.

## Runtime per live tick (20 ms)

Inside an atmosphere a vessel is always live (`sim::vessel::aerothermal`):
1. The air at the tick's start, from the body whose atmosphere the vessel is in: density, temperature, speed of sound, mean free path; the wind **w** = v_air − v with v_air = v_body + ω_body × r. q = ½ρ|w|², M = |w|/a, Kn = λ/D_b (D_b the bake's maximum body diameter: the diameter of the disc of the largest projected area over the grid's directions, as Wilmoth's bridging was fitted; until 2026-10-26 the bounding-sphere diameter, 2.2× larger for the test craft). Frozen for the tick.
2. Force and moment from `aero::aero_forces` at the flow direction in body axes, by regime:
   - hypersonic (M ≥ 5): modified Newtonian, Cp,max from the Rayleigh pitot formula at the effective γ of equilibrium air behind the shock at the flight speed (`aero::air::real_gas_gamma`);
   - subsonic (M < 0.8): pressure drag q·Cd₀·A_proj along the flow, the Newtonian pressure distribution scaled to it (the centre of pressure is the shape's); transonic: the wave drag of the direction (baked, interpolated) joins it, smoothstep from none at M 0.8 to full at M 1, capped at 0.5 × the projected area (slender-body theory diverges for blunt shapes; Apollo's and a sphere's drag rise ≈ 0.5); M 1.2–5: linear in M to the hypersonic values;
   - skin friction in every continuum regime (`aero::friction` over `aero::boundary`): each cell not facing downstream more steeply than 45° takes the shear τ·A along the flow's tangential direction t = d − n(n·d); run length x from the most upstream point of the cells along the flow, each cell taking its layer over its extent x ± h along the flow (h from the cell's second moment of area, baked). The layer's **edge state** is the Newtonian tangent wedge (Tauber, NASA TP-2914 eqs. 57–58): u_e = V·cos θ, T_e/T∞ = 1 + (γ−1)/2·M²·sin²θ (the normal kinetic energy becomes enthalpy), p_e = p∞ + q·Cp,max·f·sin²θ on exposed windward cells; the free stream on a plate along the flow. Laminar Blasius c_f = 0.664·√(C*/Re_x,e) with Eckert's reference temperature at the wall's temperature (averaged exactly over the cell), turbulent White's c_f = 0.455/ln²(0.06·Re_x) through Van Driest II with the wall's temperature; **transition** where the momentum-thickness Reynolds number Re_θ = 0.664·√(C*·Re_x) reaches max(469.5, 350·M_e) (a smooth plate's onset at Re_x 5·10⁵; the Re_θ/M_e correlation of the Shuttle's flight data), fully turbulent at twice that run length, the Dhawan–Narasimha intermittency between; the wall adiabatic unless the skin's temperatures are passed (`aero_forces_wall`); Re from ρV/μ, Sutherland viscosity;
   - rarefied: free molecular (fully accommodating, cold wall: 2·q·A along the flow, which includes the tangential momentum), bridged in Kn by Wilmoth's sin² formula.
3. **Rotation:** the moment about the centre of mass (τ − r_cm × F), plus the parachute's (its drag along the flow at its mount), is evaluated at each RK4 stage of the rotation (`rigid::tick_with`: it depends on the attitude). The craft trims itself: the test craft falls base first.
4. **Translation:** the force's component along the flow becomes a drag area (plus the parachute's), so the drag stays velocity- and density-dependent inside the tick's adaptive integration; the rest (lift) is a constant acceleration over the tick.
5. **Heating:** q̇_stag by Sutton–Graves with the direction's nose radius, plus the shock layer's radiation by Tauber–Sutton where the atmosphere names it (`radiative_heating: Some(Earth)`; `vessel::aerothermal::stagnation_flux`); each cell gets q̇_stag·max(f·sin^1.5θ, 3 %)·A (f its exposure); plus sunlight: the stars' flux at the vessel, dimmed by eclipses of body spheres (`sim::light`, the rule the game's lighting uses too), absorbed ε·S·cosθ·f per cell (self-shadowing through the same bake). **Over the whole body** (D085, `thermal::cell_heat_flow`, built 2026-10-26; the vessel still calls `cell_heat` until `vessel::aerothermal` passes the flow and the skin temperatures): each attached cell's boundary layer (the friction's, `aero::boundary`) gives laminar and turbulent heating by Colburn's analogy, St = (c_f/2)·Pr^−⅔, q̇ = St·ρ_e·u_e·c_p·(T_aw − T_w) at the skin's own temperature; laminar, a cell takes the smaller of its plate value and the larger of the nose law q̇_stag·(1 − T_w/T₀)·f·sin θ (the cosine of the body angle, Tauber's fig. 5) and a plate along the flow (at most q̇_stag); cells facing the flow beyond the sonic point (sin θ > 0.7, blended ±0.1: a fin's leading edge, a step) are stagnation regions of their own and take the nose law; turbulent never below laminar; across transition (1−γ)·laminar + γ·turbulent; at least 3 % of q̇_stag (leeward, separated). The stagnation flux is capped at the free-molecular ½ρV³ and bridged to the free-molecular flux ½ρV³·f·sin θ by Wilmoth's function of λ/Rn. Walls above the recovery temperature are cooled by the air.
6. **Thermal step:** backward Euler for the network (cells, neighbours, the interior volume nodes; see "Thermal network"), with the engine's wall heat (its share of ½·F·vₑ this tick) into the nozzle's cells by area (D077) and the nodes' capacities for the propellant aboard; solved per cell exactly (Newton on the quartic) and per node linearly, in Gauss–Seidel sweeps (cells in order, then nodes) until no temperature changes by more than 1e-13 relative (at most 128; 3 per live tick). Radiation to 2.725 K (planet IR, albedo and convective cooling later). Stable at any step.
7. **Limits:** after each tick, the hottest cell above `skin_max_k`, else the hottest node above `internal_max_k`, destroys the vessel: `Phase::Crashed { cause: Destruction::Overheat { at: Cell(i) | Node(j), temperature } }`, the wreck fixed to the body whose air it was in; not in debug mode (D064).

Measured (release, 512 cells, 110 nodes): a whole live tick in the atmosphere (aero, heating, integration) 74 µs; of which aero forces 10 µs per evaluation (5 per tick; skin friction 6.5 µs of it), cell heating 4 µs, thermal step 24 µs.

## Coasts and rails warp

- **Coasts are above every atmosphere.** A coast segment marks where it first descends below an atmosphere's top (`Segment::entry`, by bisection like contact); the vessel goes live there. The rest of the segment is a prediction, kept for display and the landing prediction. A live vessel starts coasting only 1 km above every atmosphere (hysteresis), with the engine off and away from surfaces. So the stored trajectory stays the truth where it is flown.
- **Coast drag** stays in the force model, attitude-independent (the design's mean drag area, plus the parachute's), so a segment remains a pure function of its start state: it acts only in predictions through an atmosphere (above the top the density is zero).
- **Thermal lattice:** while coasting the temperatures step on a lattice from the vessel's thermal epoch (sunlight and radiation only). Its spacing follows the state: twice the skin's shortest radiative time constant C/(4εσT³), between 1 s and **600 s** (600 s is a third of the hull's ≈ 1,600 s at 290 K; the interior's is days). A fixed 600 s lattice held a nozzle bell hot from a burn at 1400 K for 600 s after cutoff (its time constant there is ≈ 5 s), then overshot to 521 K where it should be near 330 K; with the spacing from the state it cools on steps of seconds (measured 1116 K at 10 s, 890 K at 30 s, 565 K at 136 s; a bare plate by hand ~1020, 830, 500 K) and a cold craft keeps 600 s (bench_fleet unchanged, 15 ms per frame at 10⁶×). Each solve takes the attitude and position at its lattice epoch (the attitude is advanced to each evaluated lattice point, which splits the frame like any frame boundary), so 60 fps and one jump give the same bits (tested, tumbling and holding attitude).
  - **Orbit-averaged sunlight** (`vessel::aerothermal::coast_sunlight`): on a bound orbit clear of its body with a period of at most a day, a coast step's sunlight is the flux at the vessel times the osculating orbit's **sunlit fraction** behind the body it orbits (`light::orbit_sunlit_fraction`: cylindrical shadow, boundaries bisected between 24 samples in eccentric anomaly and timed by Kepler's equation; the penumbra and other occluders are left out; tested against the beta-angle formula and brute-force sampling of an e = 0.3 orbit). Otherwise the sunlight at the point, eclipses included. This is the orbit-average heat load of spacecraft thermal practice: coast temperatures are orbit averages, and **the eclipse swing of the skin in low orbit (tens of kelvin for the test craft) is not shown**; live ticks keep instantaneous sunlight.
  - **Error-controlled solves:** a lattice point is skipped (the next solve spans it; backward Euler is stable at any step) while the sunlight in body axes is within 1 % of the last solve's, no planned burn heats the span, and the last solve's rate of change says no temperature has moved more than 0.5 K since (at most a day). While the attitude is still (no rotation, no control ticks), lattice points are passed without even evaluating the sunlight except every hour. Coast solves converge to 1e-5 relative (≈ 3 mK; live ticks 1e-13).
  - A coast that ends between lattice points steps to its end. Planned burns on rails heat the nozzle too: the burns the trajectory holds are recorded (start, end, heat) before it drops them, and each solve takes their heat over its span, so the result does not depend on the frames (tested); a burn never lets a step be skipped. The burn's heat is spread over the solve's span (up to the coast lattice), so the nozzle's peak is not resolved on rails and the mount's gain is overestimated (a 95 s burn: +6.5 K on rails against ≈ +2 K live, estimated). Skin steps are over-relaxed at coast steps (ω 1.3 below 300 s, 1.6 above).
  - **Work per frame is bounded:** `Vessel::advance(.., budget)` spends at most `budget` coast integration steps and `budget` thermal units (a lattice point 1, a solve 30 ≈ 200 µs); out of budget, the vessel stops at its last lattice point and the game holds its clock back (compute-limited), continuing next frame with the same result (tested: 44 budgeted calls equal one call).
  - Measured (`examples/bench_fleet`, release, 11 test craft in 300–500 km orbits holding attitude, 10⁶ s of simulated time per frame, coasts integrated ahead): the thermal and attitude work costs 12 ms per 10⁶ s per vessel once settled (285 ms for all 11 in the first 10⁶ s after launch, from a uniform 290 K), against 120 ms per vessel for integrating the same coast; before (60 s lattice, instantaneous eclipses) 2.7 s per vessel. At 10⁶× and 60 fps that is ≈ 0.2 ms per vessel per frame. A tumbling vessel solves at every lattice point (≈ 250 µs per 600 s).
- Landed and crashed vessels keep their temperatures (ground heat exchange later).

## Hitbox

The contact points of §3e (gear feet and hull convex-hull cells) are the hitbox against terrain. Vessel–vessel contact waits for docking and parts.

The model computes surface pressure and friction per cell; it never solves the flow. What it does and does not capture, as built on 2026-09-26, and what closes each gap:

## Thermal network (D065, revised)

Skin cells over the surface, and interior volume nodes on a coarse grid (≈1 m for the test craft), each with the heat capacity of the mass inside it; conduction between neighbours and between nodes and the skin above them; heat sources (engine losses, reactor waste heat, equipment) into the nodes that contain them; coolant loops, radiators and heat pipes as explicit links. One implicit step for the whole network, stable at any time step.

**As built** (`sim::craft::volume`, `sim::craft::design`, `sim::thermal`): cubes of `node_size` over the craft's bounds, each sampled at 4³ points; a sample is inside when a primitive's solid contains it (not the mesh: no leaks at seams). A node wherever a cube has an inside sample: its volume is the inside share, its centre their centroid; face neighbours conduct k·A/d with A the face times the smaller inside share. The test craft: 110 nodes, 247 links, 58 m³. Each skin cell couples to the node a quarter cube beneath its centroid (else the nearest node: legs and feet). Capacities per step: the dry interior spread by volume, plus the propellant where it sits (the tank's samples fill from its base to the level; the node capacities follow the fill as it drains). The engine's wall heat goes into the nozzle's cells, which couple to the node a quarter cube inside its mount (D077). `ThermalNetwork::step` takes per-node heat (W) and capacities (J/K); a generic per-node heat input is there for reactors and electronics. Tests: energy is conserved with sources off (20 ms to 1 h steps); a heated node under a radiating cell settles at the analytic T_c + Q/G; 1 h steps stay bounded and converge; a five-minute burn brings the nozzle to its radiative equilibrium (1402 K) and warms the mount's node ~7 K, the most of any node; live and planned burns give the same bits at any frame rate.

## Tests (the specification)

- Sphere: Cd ≈ 0.47 subsonic (Re ~1e5–1e6), ≈ 0.92 at M 10 (Newtonian with Cp,max 1.84: Cd = Cp,max/2), → 2.1 free-molecular (Kn ≫ 1).
- Cone: Newtonian normal force against the analytic sin²θ integral.
- Real gas: Cp,max at 11 km/s in 1.9–2.0; an Apollo-like capsule's hypersonic L/D ≈ 0.3 at α 20°.
- Wave drag: a Sears–Haack body's baked wave drag against 9π·A²/(2L²); a blunt capsule's drag rise against Apollo's, a slender body's far smaller.
- Lift: a slender cone-cylinder's normal-force slope against slender-body theory and Allen–Perkins at 10°; a flat-plate wing's lift slope against Helmbold and supersonic linear theory, antisymmetric in α, lower along its span, stalled beyond 30°; the test craft has no fins.
- Skin friction: a thin flat plate against Blasius (laminar), the integrated local laws with the transition zone, Prandtl–Schlichting's mixed plate and White's turbulent plate; Van Driest II's ratio at M 2 and 5, its low-Mach limit, hot walls lowering it; a cylinder along the flow has friction on its side only.
- Boundary layer (`aero::boundary`, `tests/aero_boundary.rs`): the edge from free stream to stagnation; transition at its criterion, γ 0.99 at twice the run; STS-1's transition front and laminar and turbulent heating on the windward centreline (Tauber fig. 14), the rise across transition.
- Atmosphere: Earth's table against the US Standard Atmosphere 1976 at 0–1,000 km (temperature, pressure, density, speed of sound, mean free path, molar mass).
- Stability: a blunt capsule with its CoM forward trims heat-shield first; moved aft, it tumbles to the other trim.
- Heating: stagnation flux within the correlations' stated accuracy at Apollo 4 and Stardust peak-heating points; over the body (`thermal/heating_tests.rs`): a plate against Pohlhausen and Colburn, inclined plates against Tauber's closed forms, a hemisphere against the cosine law, hot walls, the free-molecular limit; Tauber–Sutton through its published f(V) points, Stardust's radiative share, the convective/radiative crossover of a 4.5 m nose; a single isolated cell relaxes to (q̇/εσ)^¼; conduction conserves energy.
- Determinism: bake hash stable; chunked = single pass for a live descent; save/load mid-descent continues bit for bit; the coast thermal lattice at 60 fps equals one jump.
- Debug mode: no destruction above the limits.
- In flight (`crates/sim/tests/aerothermal.rs`): an entry from a 200 × 40 km orbit burns the test craft up (no heat shield, D064); in debug mode it lands, having peaked far above its limit; a craft at rest in sunlight settles; drag in the upper atmosphere decays an orbit, live and in the coast model.
