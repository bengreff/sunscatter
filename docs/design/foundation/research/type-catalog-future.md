# Part and component type catalog: future eras (research)

Status: research input, not canon. Extends [type-catalog.md](type-catalog.md) (present-day: 90 part types, 181 component types) forward in time to pressure-test [part-schema.md](../part-schema.md)'s rules 1-38 against the technologies named in [scope-2026-11.md](../../../plans/scope-2026-11.md) 2, 5.1, 5.2. Purpose: show how far the schema reaches before it needs something new, and list what breaks, ranked by how soon it bites. This is not a database of real hardware, and nothing here overrides the present-day catalog or any settled rule.

Three eras, as given: **near** 2035-2060 (current NASA/ESA/NIAC roadmap technology), **mid** 2060-2200 (fusion-era, needs real but undemonstrated physics), **far** 2200+ (interstellar, needs only known physics pushed to its limits). Nothing here needs unknown physics: no warp, no reactionless drive, no kugelblitz. Where a named technology fails that test (the Bussard ramjet), it is excluded and the exclusion is argued, not just asserted.

Columns match the present-day catalog (id, what, key params, mounts/ports, component slots, examples, fields), plus **era** and a **schema fit** column answering the task's question per type: does it fit part-schema.md's rules as they stand, or does it need a new model family, mount kind, domain solver, or science field. "Fits" means the existing rules (class node per size regime and alternative design per rule 15, the engine-interior-table pattern of rule 16, the deformable-part family of rule 8, articulated mount surfaces of rule 19) already cover it; a new need is named plainly.

Sources are cited by name inline in the tables' examples column and gathered in section 7.

## 5. Far-term part types (2200+)

**Bussard ramjet — excluded.** Not included below. The idea (an unshielded magnetic scoop collecting interstellar hydrogen as fusion fuel, Bussard 1960) needs no unknown physics by itself, but it fails on known physics: proton-proton fusion's cross-section is too low for a ramjet-scale reactor to burn scooped protium at any practical scoop radius, and the scoop's own drag (ram pressure on the collected but largely unburned gas) exceeds the thrust available from fusing the tiny fraction that does react (Whitmire 1975 first raised the drag problem; Fogg 1989 and later analyses found no fix without either a different fuel the interstellar medium doesn't actually contain in useful quantity, or scoop radii in the thousands of km, which then run into the same big-parts and tensioned-structure limits as the orbital ring, for a drive that still doesn't close its own thrust budget). It is left out on these physical grounds, not because the schema can't express a large magnetic scoop — it can, as a size-regime node of `power.sail_magnetic_plasma`'s field-generating pattern.

## 7. Gaps and schema stress

Ranked by how early it bites (near era first), not by how exotic it sounds. Several far-era items turn out to be schema successes (fusion, antimatter propulsion, beamed sails, interstellar hulls all extend rules 15 and 16 cleanly); the real stress points start much sooner.

1. **G2 — No aero/lift part family exists at all.** The present-day catalog has no wing, no propeller/rotor, no fixed control surface — every present-day aero surface is a grid fin, a heatshield, or a parachute, none of which generate lift the way a wing or rotor does. This bites at `aircraft.titan_rotorcraft` and `aero.wing_fixed`, needed for NASA's Dragonfly (launching 2028, arriving at Titan 2034) — inside the near era's own window, and arguably before sunscatter's own first release if the owner wants Dragonfly playable. Needs: a new part family, not just new types. The aero/thermal cell model (3.2) already computes surface loads, so the *solver* mostly extends; the *part type* (a lift-generating surface with its own mount and failure modes) does not exist yet.
2. **G1 — The deformable/gossamer part family (settled rule 8) is approved but not built, and the near era already needs it in three different shapes.** Rule 8 says a never-rigid part gets a deformable-part model family "when the first one is needed (Dragon's chutes)." The near era needs it for a large multi-layer sunshield (`thermal.sunshield_deployable`), a ribbed mesh reflector (`comms.antenna_mesh_deployable_large`), and a two-phase inflate-then-rigidize structure, before Dragon's chutes are even the hardest case. Needs: build it now, and extend it to cover (a) a membrane under essentially uniform load (a sail, a sunshield), (b) a ribbed/tensioned mesh (a reflector, an orbital ring's cable loop — see G1-mid below), and (c) a phase change from deformable to rigid mid-mission.
3. **G3 — No continuous, unbounded rotary mount kind.** Rule 19's articulated mount surfaces (hinge, gimbal, slider) all have "range and stiffness" — a bounded travel. A propeller, a rotor, and a rotating-habitat ring all turn through 360 degrees continuously and indefinitely, which is a different kind of joint (continuous angular rate and torque, not a bounded position). This bites at `aero.propeller_rotor_blade_assembly` and `habitat.rotating_ring_module`, both near era. Needs: a new articulated mount-surface kind alongside hinge/gimbal/slider.
4. **G5 — No buoyancy/aerostat domain.** Floating at a density-matched altitude in a planetary atmosphere (Venus balloon) or underwater (an icy-moon hydrobot) is neither the rigid-body-plus-load-path structural model, nor the lumped fluid network, nor the aero/thermal cell model — it needs its own lift-from-buoyancy balance against a surrounding fluid. Bites at `aerostat.venus_balloon_platform`, near era. Needs: a new domain model (or a well-argued extension of the fluid graph to carry an external, not just internal, fluid).
5. **G8 — No melting-boundary thermal domain.** Ablation (rule: the present-day catalog's own named gap) removes a craft's own material as it heats; a cryobot does the reverse — it melts a path *through* the environment ahead of it. Both are receding-boundary problems, but in opposite directions, and neither fits the lumped thermal node network as-is. Bites at `probe.cryobot_ice_penetrator`, near era (a funded mission concept, not just a placeholder). Needs: a domain extension to the thermal solver, probably shared code with the ablation extension once both exist.
6. **G4 — No running-attachment mount kind.** Every mount surface in the schema sits at a fixed position on a part's exterior (rule: "what a part type declares," mounting surfaces). A tether climber, a cryobot's trailing communications reel, and (mid era) a rotovator's tip grapple all attach to a point that moves continuously along an extended flexible part's length. First appears near era (tethered spin-gravity, cryobot tether) and becomes load-bearing mid era (rotovators, momentum-exchange tethers). Needs: a new mount kind distinct from a fixed-position surface.
7. **G9 — Granular mechanics has no domain, and ISRU multiplies the problem.** Already named as a gap in the present-day catalog (rover wheels, excavation) with no clear field owner; near-era ISRU plants (regolith excavation, hopper feed, sintering) make this load-bearing for an entire family of parts rather than an edge case. Needs: either an explicit granular-mechanics extension to F5 or F12 (as the present-day catalog already recommended), built out now rather than deferred.
8. **G6 — No way to represent a population of many identical small parts.** First appears near era as SBSP's thousands of heliostat mirror elements, and becomes existential far era as a Dyson swarm's millions of collector elements. The performance budget (5,000 parts per craft, section 3.5 of the scope doc) was sized for one complex vehicle, not a million-member swarm; treating each element as its own tracked part doesn't scale, but collapsing them into one aggregate object loses the "it's made of real, individually failable parts" property the rest of the schema insists on. Needs: a population/aggregate representation that is new to the game layer as much as the physics schema — likely a statistical envelope over a template part, with individual parts only materialized when something touches one directly.
9. **G7 — Building a part during play, not just designing it once.** In-space additive manufacturing and ISRU-built landing pads mean a part can be *constructed* by another part as a gameplay action, not only produced from a recipe after a design program (section 5.2's four-layer science system describes designing and building, not one part fabricating another in the field). Near era. Needs: a build-in-play path alongside the existing design-program-then-recipe path; likely a game-layer addition more than a physics one, but it touches the part/component schema wherever a printed part needs the same node and parameter machinery as a factory-built one.
10. **G10 — No shared-field graph.** Active magnetic radiation shielding needs several parts' superconducting coils to sum into one coherent deflecting field around a habitat; this is not a per-part property the way thermal view factors or fluid pressure are, and none of the five existing system graphs (fluid, power, data, thermal, structural) models "several sources summing into one shared field region." Mid era (`shield.active_magnetic_radiation_shield`). Needs: either a sixth graph type or an explicit multi-source extension to the power/structural graphs.
11. **G11 — No guideway mount kind.** A mass-driver track and its payload sled need a carrier to ride a continuous, powered track and release at the end — distinct from a fixed joint (rule 19's hinge/gimbal/slider) and from ordinary separation (rule 9, which assumes two parts that were mated, not a part riding a track it's never mated to in the usual sense). Mid era (`launcher.mass_driver_track_segment`). Needs: a new mount kind.
12. **G12 — No failure mode categorically above "burst."** Rule 11's broken-state model (a set number of mass-lump segments, possibly with an explosion) assumes the stored energy is bounded by the part's own pressure or structural strain energy. An antimatter containment failure releases the full rest-mass energy of whatever is stored — many orders of magnitude beyond any chemical or pressure-vessel failure the schema has priced in. Mid era (`vessel.antimatter_storage_pod`). Needs: an explicit new failure-mode category, not just a bigger number plugged into the existing one.
13. **G13 — Relativistic impact/erosion has no domain.** The present-day catalog's named hypervelocity-impact gap (SP-8042-style shock/spall physics) assumes non-relativistic closing speeds. An interstellar cruise at a meaningful fraction of light speed changes both the energy scale and the physics (relativistic kinetic energy, not Newtonian) of every dust or gas-atom impact. Far era (`shield.relativistic_ism_deflector`). Needs: a relativistic extension to the impact-physics domain; the craft-level astrodynamics already carries special relativity (scope-2026-11.md section 5, "the interstellar physics carried from day one"), so the missing piece is specifically the impact/erosion physics, not the kinematics.

**Also not a gap, but worth naming:** the big-parts question part-schema.md leaves open (9.6, "the maximum size per part type before a part must be split or gain cell-level failure") gets asked again and again from the mid era on — a rotovator tether, an orbital ring segment, an interstellar cruise hull, a Dyson swarm element's very different problem (too small and too numerous, not too large) — and is worth settling before mid-era design work starts, since G1, G4, G10 and G13 all lean on whatever that answer turns out to be.

**What did not break.** The class-node-per-size-regime pattern (rule 15) carried every "just bigger" case cleanly: depot tanks, MW-class reactors and radiators, large trusses, big docking standards, and even the orbital ring's planetary-scale segments are all alternative-design or size-regime nodes of a family that already exists. The engine-interior-table pattern (rule 16) carried every new propulsion physics cleanly from NTR through MCF/ICF fusion to antimatter beam-core and catalyzed drives — the table's contents change, not the part/component split around it. The "thruster pods become parts" rule (settled 7) scaled without edits from an RCS quad down to a Dyson-swarm element's station-keeping thruster and up to an orbital ring's stabilization pod. And "decoupling is always a component" (settled 9) held for every new separation case tried, including an antimatter pod's emergency jettison.
