# Research: flight UI and information architecture (2026-10-27)

For the owner's "I have no idea where the essential info is" (interview, verbatim in the task). Scope: staging bar, part live menus, RCS menu, g-load, time warp while burning, menu redesign, systems-screen layering, Mac keys. Grounded in `docs/decisions.md` D100–D108, `docs/technical/flight-menus.md`, `docs/technical/systems-screen.md`, `docs/technical/ship-systems.md`, and the current code (`crates/game/src/interface/`, `navball/`, `staging.rs`, `systems_screen/`).

**Running principle, stated once:** D106 — a value with no sensor is a dash, not a guess, and never wrong; estimates (burn time, Δv, impact) are always labelled as predictions over the stored trajectory with no further input (rule 4, `docs/owners.md`). Every mod mechanism below is filtered through that: KSP mods routinely show omniscient numbers (perfect dV, perfect fuel state, perfect reliability %) that Sunscatter cannot show on a flown craft without a sensor and a reason. Flagged inline as **(sensor gap)** wherever a mod's number would need an instrument Sunscatter's parts may not have.

## 1. Staging and per-stage info

**Stock KSP (1.6+, "To Vee or not To Vee"):** added per-stage Δv and a Δv app, shown in the editor and in flight on the stage icons themselves — a small number under each icon. [KSP 1.6 devblog](https://kerbaldevteam.tumblr.com/post/181273518219/kerbal-space-program-16-to-vee-or-not-to-vee). The stock calculation assumes a *fixed* stage boundary (everything in a stage burns together, mass drops at the stage event) and vacuum Isp; it does not model crossfeed well — it just sums the stage's own tanks plus anything reachable through an enabled fuel line, by the editor's "part is still attached" graph, not real-time flow simulation.

**Kerbal Engineer Redux (KER):** the model used across most realism players. It *actually simulates* the resource drain stage-by-stage (not a closed-form estimate): it walks the fuel-flow graph (same priority/crossfeed rules the game itself uses — tanks have an integer flow priority, equal-priority tanks drain together, `ENABLE_CROSSFEED` toggles whether a part conducts flow through itself) and burns down a virtual copy of the vessel stage by stage to get Δv, burn time and TWR per stage. Known failure mode, confirmed in the KER issue tracker and forum: **if a stage's engine can draw fuel through crossfeed from a tank that will be jettisoned with a *later* stage, KER (and stock) cannot tell "this stage's intended burn" from "fuel that happens to be reachable right now."** It silently credits the stage with fuel that will physically leave with the next stage, understating or overstating Δv depending on direction. [Forum: flow priority](https://steamcommunity.com/app/220200/discussions/0/2741975115076019610/), [GitHub #92](https://github.com/jrbudda/KerbalEngineer/issues/92). **Lesson:** a stage's "which tanks feed it" question is fundamentally ambiguous from the attachment graph alone — it needs the *planned* separation event, not just current flow connectivity. Sunscatter already has the answer in hand: `Vessel`'s own stage sequence (D107, `ShipState::stages`) is authoritative, so "which tanks drain in this stage" is computable exactly as "simulate forward from here, assuming no further stage commands, until this stage's own separation/shutdown orders fire" — not a graph heuristic. This is strictly better than KER's problem because Sunscatter's stage list is a *plan*, not an attachment-graph guess.

**MechJeb:** shows Δv per stage in its info window (not the stock stage icons) and is the most commonly cross-checked source for "is this estimate even right," because its simulation is closer to KER's forward-burn method than stock's closed-form one. [Forum: MechJeb delta-v per stage](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/156259-mechjeb-delta-v-per-stage/).

**Better Burn Time (BBT):** fixes stock's main complaint — stock shows burn time from `Δv / current acceleration`, which is wrong the instant the ship's mass starts dropping during the burn (the real burn is shorter, since TWR rises as propellant burns). BBT integrates burn time properly: time to burn a given Δv at full throttle accounting for the mass decreasing during the burn itself, not just a snapshot acceleration. [Burn-time calculator thread](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/156258-burn-time-calculator/); summary of the fix: "if you have calculated how much fuel you need to burn from your start mass and ISP, you have already taken the increase of acceleration into account" — i.e. solve the rocket equation for burn duration, don't divide by instantaneous accel.

**RealFuels (ullage/ignitions in the stack):** RealFuels adds an ullage status and remaining-ignitions count that read on the engine's own right-click part menu and are exposed to scripts (`ENGINE:IGNITIONS` in kOS returns remaining ignitions, or -1 for unlimited: [kOS Engine docs](https://ksp-kos.github.io/KOS/structures/vessels/engine.html)). These are *not* folded into the stock stage-icon display in vanilla RealFuels — they live on the part, which is exactly Sunscatter's split (stage bar = essentials, part card = everything about that part). **Lesson taken, not rejected:** ignitions-remaining and ullage state belong on the engine's **part card**, not cluttering the stage bar; the stage bar should show only the aggregate "this stage still works" signal (e.g. an icon tinted amber if any engine in it is low on ignitions or the propellant is unsettled), with the detail one click away.

**KSP2:** per-stage Δv exists behind an extra click (hidden by default, toggled from an arrow next to the Launch/stage button), and was infamous for being *unreliable* — numbers that didn't match reality during EA. [KSP2 forum thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/954850/discussions/0/4033600999066567595/). **Explicit lesson for Sunscatter:** an estimate that is sometimes wrong is worse than no estimate. D106's "never shows something wrong" principle is the correct antidote — if the estimate's assumptions (no further input, current trajectory) stop holding, the number must either recompute every tick (cheap, since it's a closed-form burn to empty) or visibly flag as stale, never silently drift from truth.

**How Sunscatter should decide "which tanks feed this stage" and "how long it lasts," concretely:**
1. Tanks belonging to this stage = the result of a **virtual forward simulation**: run the real plumbing/engine feed-logic (`sim::systems::plumbing`, `sim::systems::propulsion`) forward from now, holding controls at *zero further input* (current throttle and attitude frozen — matching the owner's own phrase "assume no input"), until the stage's own next scripted event (shutdown/separation order in `ShipState::stages`) fires or the craft runs dry. This reuses the one owner of fuel-feed math instead of inventing a second one (rule 5), and it is automatically correct about crossfeed because it's the same code that would actually run the ship.
2. Burn time = how long that simulation ran before depletion or the stage's own end order, not `Δv/accel` (BBT's fix, free improvement over stock).
3. The estimate is explicitly a projection, not telemetry — it must be labelled (e.g. a small "projected, no input" caption under the bar, matching the Orbital menu's existing "ELEMENTS · OSCULATING" disclosure pattern already in `flight-menus.md`) and must recompute whenever the actual controls or stage sequence change (it is not stored once; it's a per-frame or per-few-ticks pure function of current `ShipState`).
4. **(sensor gap)** the fraction-full bar and burn-time estimate are each individually subject to D106: if the craft has no level sensor on a tank, that tank's contribution to the stage's fuel bar is a dash/"unknown", and the stage's overall bar and time estimate should read **"unknown"**, not silently assume full or empty. This is new territory relative to any KSP mod — none of them model sensor-starved fuel gauging — and is worth flagging to the owner as added scope (see Open Questions).

## 2. Per-part live info

**Stock Part Action Window (PAW):** the right-click window. Universally criticized for exactly the owner's complaint in miniature — it is one undifferentiated list per part, grows with every module added to a part (TweakScale alone added 3–6 extra rows to *every single part in the game*, which is the canonical "clutter" complaint from the TweakScale era — threads full of "why does every tank now have a scale slider I never look at"), doesn't persist (closes when you click elsewhere, re-opens from scratch), and stacks awkwardly when several are open ("PAW stacking/overlap" is a perennial complaint topic). **Lesson: a part's live card must (a) persist while selected rather than being a transient popup, (b) show only what's essential to *this ship, now* — not every tunable the generator exposes, and (c) never grow because an unrelated system (TweakScale-equivalent) was added.**

**PartCommander:** the direct fix the community built for PAW's problems — one consolidated, persistent window listing every part's action-menu, with per-part sub-windows poppable out, position/size/selection persisted per save. [GitHub](https://github.com/seanmcdougall/PartCommander), [SpaceDock](https://spacedock.info/mod/615/Part%20Commander). This validates the owner's "ONE constant menu per part" instinct: persistence and a single consistent place to look (not re-finding the part each time) is exactly what players asked a mod to bolt on. **Taken:** the part card should be a persistent, addressable panel (open/close state remembered per part, like the existing menu system's saved section-expansion state in `settings.ron`), not a transient tooltip.

**TestFlight:** its Master Status Display (MSD) is the closest prior art to "essential stats for this part, right now": one line per TestFlight-enabled part showing accumulated flight data, current reliability-relevant status and a repair affordance, for every part on the active craft at once, flight-scene only. [GitHub wiki: MSD](https://github.com/KSP-RO/TestFlight/wiki/Master-Status-Display). Its reliability number is **synthetic** (computed from a data/time curve), not sensed — exactly what D106 forbids showing as if measured. **Lesson taken:** the *list-of-parts-with-status* pattern (MSD) is good scaffolding for the Staging bar's "icon per part, click to open its card," but TestFlight's reliability percentage itself is the wrong thing to show live in Sunscatter; D105's rated numbers (reliability score 1–10, rated life, rated temp) are *design-time* comparison numbers the organization knows about a part model, not a live "this specific part has an X% chance of failing now" readout — that would be showing something the ship cannot sense. The live card should show *measured condition* (temperature, load ratio, accumulated damage fraction if the craft can sense it) and let the player infer risk, matching real telemetry.

**Kerbalism:** the most relevant systems-UI prior art for a *multi-layer* ship. Its Monitor window aggregates vessel-wide telemetry (crew vitals, resource levels, habitat/environment) plus a per-part reliability glyph: a wrench icon, white/yellow/red for no-fault/malfunction/critical-failure. [Kerbalism GUI docs](https://kerbalism.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gui.html), [Kerbalism/Kerbalism GitHub](https://github.com/Kerbalism/Kerbalism). Its per-part sensor data literally becomes "a part's UI and a telemetry panel" — i.e. Kerbalism already treats "does this part have a sensor" as gating what's shown, structurally the same shape as D106, just not as strict (Kerbalism still leaks some omniscient numbers for playability). **Lesson:** the three-colour fault glyph (ok/degraded/critical) is a good, cheap-to-scan icon-badge convention worth reusing on the stage bar's per-part icons and the systems-screen part list, read from the part's actual `Runtime` state (`Runtime::lost`, damage fraction, D105) rather than synthesized.

**RealFuels engine PAW:** shows, on the engine itself: current thrust, current Isp, propellant mix ratio, ignitions remaining, ullage status — i.e. *exactly* the owner's "essential stats for that part on this ship right now" instinct, just for engines specifically, already in a shape close to what's wanted.

**What belongs on a live part card, synthesized from the above plus Sunscatter's own data model (`ship-systems.md`, `systems_screen.md`'s inspector groups):**

General rule: show the part's **current measured state against its own rated/limit numbers** (D105's comparison ratings), each a `value/limit` pair à la the owner's "kg/kg," each subject to D106 (dash if unsensed), nothing about *other* parts, no tuning sliders (those live in the craft file / systems-screen editors, not the live card — this avoids the TweakScale-slider-on-every-part complaint). See §"Proposed part-card content" below for the per-kind breakdown.

**One clarification worth raising with the owner:** the task says "ONE constant menu per part" — read against D106 and the systems screen's existing `inspect::details`, this likely means "one *kind* of card, reused for every part, showing only the fields relevant to that part's functions" (an engine's card shows thrust/Isp rows, a tank's card shows fill/temperature rows, nothing shows blank rows) rather than literally identical content per part. The systems screen's inspector already does this (`inspect::details`, grouped by function: tanks, engines, mechanisms, aero surfaces, components) — the live card is a trimmed, flight-safe subset of that same grouping, which also satisfies "a link to a specific screen of the systems screen" (the card's header button opens the systems screen with this part pre-selected).

## 3. RCS

**RCS Build Aid:** the canonical "show the physics, don't just toggle thrusters" mod. In the *editor* it draws colour-coded vectors: translation force (green), net torque from imbalance (red), per-thruster contribution (cyan), referenced to a chosen centre-of-mass marker (CoM/DCoM/an "average CoM" over the burn as propellant depletes). [GitHub](https://github.com/m4v/RCSBuildAid), [forum](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/33124-111x-rcs-build-aid-v106/). Its core insight: RCS *authority* is not a static number — it depends on where the CoM currently is, which moves as propellant burns, so a good RCS display must be live, not a build-time-only estimate. **MechJeb's RCS balancer** does the equivalent at runtime: it solves thruster allocation to cancel unwanted torque during a translation burn, i.e. runtime least-squares allocation, which is exactly what Sunscatter's `sim::systems::rcs::translate` already is ("the nozzles pushing along a wanted direction... give force and torque from their geometry"). **Lesson:** the RCS menu's "overrides" and "list of thrusters" should show each thruster's *current* contribution (its real-time commanded/actual force from the same allocation math flight already runs), not a build-time static table — reuse `rcs::translate`'s output, don't recompute a second approximation (rule 5).

**Real spacecraft precedent (Dragon/Shuttle/Apollo):** Dragon's touchscreen puts translation controls (arrow/±️ icons) in a dedicated corner of the HUD, separate from attitude, and only live/relevant during the phase it matters (proximity ops) — a context-gated panel, not an always-on cluster. [Space.com](https://www.space.com/spacex-crew-dragon-touchscreen-astronaut-thoughts.html), [UX writeup](https://uxdesign.cc/how-i-recreated-crew-dragons-ui-15877eddf3ed). This matches Kitten Space Agency's "gauges with contexts" idea (below, §6) and directly matches the owner's own ask: "a new RCS menu that appears only when an 'RCS' button on the navball is pressed" — i.e. context-gated by whether RCS mode is active, exactly the Dragon pattern. Apollo/Shuttle used physical rotational/translational hand controllers (RHC/THC) as separate controls from the attitude stick — precedent for Sunscatter's WASD (rotation, gimbal-authority) vs IJKL (translation, RCS-authority) split being a real, not just a KSP, convention.

**KSP's own IJKL/HN convention:** I/K = up/down, J/L = left/right, H/N = forward/back, active while the RCS "R" toggle is on, and the *same* WASDQE keys double as rotation (RCS-assisted when RCS is on) vs gimbal/aero control when it's off — i.e. KSP already does precisely what the owner asked for ("WASD use RCS as well as engine gimbals to turn"). [Keybind reference](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2158532486). **Direct conflict found in Sunscatter's existing key map** (see §8/Key map below): `L` is already the Landing/Reentry menu key and `H`/`N` are already Help and the burn-planner key. Carrying IJKL+HN over verbatim would shadow three existing single-letter flight menus. This needs the owner's decision — flagged in Open Questions, with a recommended resolution.

**Realism-target flag:** none of the above mods compute RCS thrust from anything but a flat per-thruster force number; Sunscatter already does better (`rcs::translate` draws real feed gas and gives true geometric torque per D104), so there is no simplification to resist here — just reuse what exists.

## 4. G-load

**Stock KSP g-meter:** a single scalar, "current g" (magnitude of proper acceleration / g₀), with no direction and no comparison to any limit — famously useless for deciding "is this safe," which is exactly the owner's "no idea where the essential info is" complaint in miniature. Sunscatter already computes the scalar (`navball::rules::g_load`, `crates/game/src/navball/rules.rs:214`, tested in `rules_tests.rs:221`) from the proper (non-gravitational) acceleration vector — the vector itself is already available at the call site (`proper_accel`, `navball/mod.rs:246`), just discarded down to a magnitude today.

**Real aviation g-meters:** measure load factor along the airframe's reference axis (nose-tail), not a magnitude-only number, specifically because *direction relative to the airframe* is what determines which structural/physiological limit applies — positive-g (eyeballs-down) and negative-g limits are different numbers on the same aircraft, and a sideways/lateral component matters for a different limit again. [AOPA](https://www.aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2026/june/flight-training/what-am-i-accelerometer-g-meter), [combatsim.com](http://www.combatsim.com/htm/nov99/g-limit.htm). Crucially: *a cockpit g-meter reads the load at its own mounting point, not at the part that will actually fail* — the number is a proxy, not a guarantee. **Direct parallel for Sunscatter:** D105/structure already computes a true per-joint load ratio (`structure::ratio`, "a ring joint's equivalent axial line load... against the wall's ultimate") and crew g-limits are separately modelled (`crew.rs`, NASA-STD-3001 windows by axis: +Ax, −Ax, lateral, each with its own time-dependent limit). The owner's ask — "current g-load out of the max g-load (in the direction the g's are going)" — maps cleanly onto **crew's own axis-relative limit**, not a generic airframe number, *if there is crew aboard*; for an uncrewed craft the relevant "max" is the weakest joint's load ratio, which is a different quantity already computed by structure. These are not the same number and conflating them would misrepresent what's actually being protected.

**Recommendation:** replace V/S with a g-load readout that is **axis-resolved against whichever limit currently binds** — crew's NASA-STD-3001 axis limit (with its own held-duration curve) when crew are aboard and the craft can sense its own attitude/acceleration (D106: an IMU/accelerometer part, 50 µg precision per `ship-systems.md`'s knowledge section), falling back to the structural joint-ratio's implied g (the proper acceleration at which the weakest *known* joint would reach ratio 1, today) when uncrewed or no crew-limit applies. Label clearly which one is shown (a one-word suffix: "CREW" or "STRUCT") so the number's meaning never has to be guessed — this is the same "label the estimate" discipline as the Orbital menu's "ELEMENTS · OSCULATING". **Do not** invent a third, KSP-style generic "g-limit" number with no physical referent — every mod surveyed that does this (stock KSP) is the thing players found useless, and it would contradict D105's rule 2 ("ratings shown for comparison," always tied to a real limit).

Format: `current / limit` with the proper-acceleration *direction* shown as a small vector glyph or a signed axis label (e.g. `3.2 / 4.0 g · +Ax`), consistent with the existing bar-and-ratio visual language already used for thermal (`heat_tone`, amber at 90%, red at limit) — reuse `Tone` directly (rule 5: one way to show "approaching a limit").

## 5. Time warp with engines

**Stock KSP:** refuses *physics* warp (10x and below is physics-accurate; 50x+ is on-rails and unpowered) while any engine is burning, full stop — the player must throttle to zero to warp at all once above 1x with thrust. This is the single most common "why can't I warp" complaint in the stock game, and is already explicitly **not** wanted per the owner's 2026-09-28 interview answer #6 ("Warp limits in the air: No").

**Persistent Thrust:** lets engines (specifically continuous low-thrust — ion, solar sail) keep burning *through* on-rails warp by applying thrust as a background `ΔV` integration rather than a per-tick physics force, with the explicit caveat that **throttle and attitude cannot be changed while warped** — you commit to a burn, then warp through it. [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/kerbal/ksp-mods/persistent-thrust), [GitHub](https://github.com/bld/PersistentThrust). This is close to what rule 4 already demands of Sunscatter generally (the stored trajectory is the truth; warp only resamples it) — a burn under warp has to be a **planned, committed, unchangeable-mid-warp segment**, which is exactly the shape Persistent Thrust forces, just forced by a real constraint Sunscatter should keep deliberately rather than as a technical workaround.

**Realism Overhaul / Principia:** RO explicitly has "physics warp" (a lower multiplier, still a real integration, used while burning low-thrust/high-Isp stages so a burn of real duration doesn't take real wall-clock minutes) distinct from on-rails warp. Principia's flight planner computes burns ahead of time but **does not execute them** — the player (or, implicitly, a script) still has to throttle up, hold attitude and `BURN` at the planned time; for an ion burn lasting days, the real workflow quoted by players is: queue the burn's Δv, run the engine at a fixed throttle, hold attitude, and *then* it is safe to warp on-rails because the controls are no longer changing. [Principia concepts](https://github.com/mockingbirdnest/Principia/wiki/Concepts), [GitHub issue #2347](https://github.com/mockingbirdnest/Principia/issues/2347). Cross-reference: "Principia makes high physics warp in BetterTimeWarp... laggy, so [a long burn] must be executed in on-rail high time warp" — i.e. even the most rigorous N-body mod in the ecosystem ends up needing exactly the owner's rule: **warp is fine once thrust is actually, stably producing force on a fixed plan; it is not fine while the player might still be adjusting the throttle/attitude that determines the outcome.**

**The owner's rule, restated precisely against this prior art:** "time warp should not be limited while the throttle is up but no thrust is produced; only once thrust starts." This is *stricter than* every mod surveyed in the player's favour (none of them warp-gate on thrust-not-yet-started — they all gate on "any throttle input") and *consistent with* rule 4 (once thrust is actually flowing, the trajectory segment is deterministic and warp-safe to sample at any rate; before thrust starts, nothing physical is happening yet regardless of throttle position, so there's nothing rule-4-unsafe about warping with the throttle lever up but an engine still in its start sequence or waiting on settling, D104's propulsion state machine). Concretely: gate on `propulsion` state == Running (or Starting, if ramp-up itself should also block warp — a one-line question for the owner, since a Merlin's 2 s start transient technically does produce some thrust) rather than on `Controls::throttle > 0`.

**"Time warp while burning" setup menu — what prior art suggests it should contain**, synthesizing Persistent Thrust's commit-then-warp model with RO's physics-warp tiers and Principia's "hold attitude, then warp" workflow:
- A **maximum allowed warp while thrust is live**, possibly tiered by current TWR/Isp (a long low-thrust burn can tolerate deeper warp than a short high-thrust one before the sampling error on burn-cutoff timing becomes visible — this is a numerics question for whoever owns the warp/integration code, not a UI one, but the menu is where the player picks the tradeoff).
- **What happens to attitude control during warp**: Persistent Thrust's answer (locked) is the safe default; a looser answer (SAS continues to hold a mode) is riskier for an N-body, no-SOI game like Sunscatter where attitude affects thrust direction every tick, and should probably be the harder-won option, not the default.
- **An explicit arm/commit step** before warp starts (matching Principia's flight-plan-then-execute split): the player locks in "this engine, this throttle (or this planned Δv), this attitude mode" and only then is warp permitted, so there is a clear moment the owner can audit if the result doesn't match the plan — directly serves rule 4's "chunked integration must equal single-pass" test, since this setup is effectively choosing the chunk boundary.

This is explicitly deferred per the task ("eventually a setup menu") — the finding above is scoped as input for *that* future design pass, not a thing to build now; today's task only needs the gating rule itself in the warp-refusal check.

## 6. Information architecture

**Aviation PFD "basic T":** attitude indicator centred (the hub of a radial scan pattern), airspeed left, altitude/vertical-speed right, heading below — a layout proven over decades specifically because pilots scan it in a fixed hub-and-spoke pattern and *muscle memory for where a number lives* is itself the safety feature (you don't hunt for the number, you already know where to look). [SKYbrary PFD](https://skybrary.aero/articles/primary-flight-display-pfd). **This is the single most directly applicable lesson to the owner's actual complaint.** Sunscatter's `flight-menus.md` design already gets half of this right (fixed places: time/warp top-left, menu bar top-centre, navball cluster bottom-centre, D080) — the gap is that the *navball cluster itself* has grown organically (speed, altitude, V/S, Ap/Pe, SAS, throttle, state line all in one place) without the basic-T's discipline of "only what's needed for the *one* task this cluster serves, at a fixed position for that one thing." The stage bar at the screen's right edge is a good opportunity to apply basic-T discipline fresh: one fixed vertical band, read top-to-bottom in stage order (matching the existing stage-numbering convention, newest/next stage nearest the thing it affects), each stage a fixed-height row so the eye learns "stage 3 is always the third row from here," not a variable-height list that reflows.

**Orbiter's MFDs:** two reconfigurable multi-function squares plus a HUD, colour-coded by reference (green = own vessel, yellow = target, grey = reference body, red = warning, blue = auxiliary) — the same colour discipline Sunscatter's `interface::theme` already follows (dim labels, monospace values, WARN/alert colours). [OrbiterWiki MFD](https://www.orbiterwiki.org/wiki/MFD). Orbiter's real lesson for Sunscatter is *not* its button layout (keyboard/mouse driven multi-mode panels, pre-dates egui-style docks) but its separation of concerns: each MFD page is one *mode* (orbit, surface, map, deorbit, entry — the "ullage" repo's PR title literally mirrors Sunscatter's own menu names) with no cross-mode clutter, reinforcing rule 8 and the existing "no submenus, one task per menu" redesign the owner is already describing — Orbiter is 20-year-old prior art for exactly that discipline.

**Kitten Space Agency ("gauges"):** draggable, resizable, independently-toggleable HUD widgets with **context gating** — a gauge declares the conditions under which it's relevant (burn planned, engines present, in atmosphere, etc.) and is simply absent otherwise, rather than present-but-greyed-out. [UI widgets wiki](https://kittenspaceagency.wiki.gg/wiki/UI_widgets). This is good precedent specifically for the owner's RCS-menu ask ("appears only when an RCS button is pressed") and for Landing/Reentry's existing atmosphere-gated sections — KSA validates context-gating as a first-class UI primitive worth generalizing rather than a one-off special case.

**Children of a Dead Earth:** the cautionary tale. A scientifically rigorous simulation whose UI exposed raw sliders and numbers for velocity-matching and inclination-matching by eye, with the community's own retrospective suggesting the game needed "a real spaceflight computer" layer between the physics and the player rather than making the player *be* the computer. [CoaDE realism blog](https://childrenofadeadearth.wordpress.com/2016/04/15/how-realistic-is-it-actually/comment-page-1/). **Direct lesson for Sunscatter, which has an even harder physics model (full N-body, no patched conics):** realism in the *simulation* does not require realism-as-tedium in the *controls*; the burn planner (already built per D076/interview-2026-09-28, draggable maneuver nodes) is the correct layer to keep doing this work, and the Orbital menu should keep surfacing its *output* (Δv, time, duration) rather than ever asking the player to eyeball-match raw numbers the way CoaDE did.

**Dragon's touchscreen / Falcon 9 webcast telemetry:** both put a small number of always-visible essentials (velocity, altitude, a simple status strip) in a fixed top band, with everything else behind explicit taps into named subsystem pages — structurally identical to the docks-plus-cluster model already chosen in D080, which suggests the existing architecture is sound and the complaint is about *cluster growth and stage/part discoverability specifically*, not the dock/menu system itself. No architecture change is indicated here beyond what's already proposed by the owner (the stage bar, the part card, trimming the Propulsion menu).

**Synthesis / recommendation:** keep D080's docks-and-cluster model (it already matches the aviation-PFD and Orbiter-MFD "fixed place, one task" lessons); add the stage bar as a *third* always-on fixed-position cluster (alongside time/warp and the navball cluster) rather than folding it into the right dock, since "always visible, no click needed" is the owner's explicit ask and matches how real missions keep stage/propellant status permanently in view (webcast telemetry strip); gate the new RCS menu by RCS-mode-on (KSA/Dragon precedent); and apply the basic-T "fixed row = fixed meaning" discipline to the stage bar's layout specifically, since that's the one new surface being built from scratch.

## 7. Systems screen / layered craft view

Sunscatter's systems screen (`systems_screen.md`, built) already implements the layer-switching craft view the owner asked for (Physical/Structural/Thermal/Plumbing/Electrical/Data/Health), coloring the craft's *own* parts per layer rather than hand-drawn schematics — this was an explicit, already-made decision (`systems-screen.md`'s "Prior work" table: "Layers on one view of the craft," taken from mission-control-style one-display-per-system thinking, rejecting hand-drawn schematics). The research questions below are about *refining*, not introducing, this.

**Stock thermal overlay (F11):** a single global heat-map tint (red cold → white glowing) over every part at once, no numeric readout, toggled independently of any menu — i.e. a *layer*, exactly Sunscatter's model, but numberless. [DefKey KSP shortcuts](https://defkey.com/kerbal-space-program-ksp-shortcuts). Sunscatter's thermal layer already goes further (blue→green→yellow→red against each part's own limit, D106-gated), which is correctly ahead of this prior art — no change indicated, just confirms the colour-ramp approach is the industry default for a reason (fast visual triage before reading exact numbers).

**Hot Spot (community thermal mod):** added skin vs. internal temperature distinction and thermal *rate* (heating/cooling trend), not just instantaneous value, to the overlay. [GitHub Apokee/HotSpot](https://github.com/Apokee/HotSpot). Sunscatter's thermal layer already separates skin cell vs. interior node (`systems-screen.md`'s thermal bullet) — the one thing not yet mentioned anywhere in the docs is a *rate* (heating/cooling trend arrow), which could be a cheap, high-value addition to the thermal layer and to the Landing/Reentry menu's SKIN MAX row (a "rising fast" flag matters more during entry than the instantaneous number) — worth floating to the owner as a small addition, not a redesign.

**Ferram Aerospace Research (FAR) aero overlay:** vessel-wide (not per-part) force/moment arrows (lift, drag, pitching moment), with a documented UX lesson — early versions showed every arrow including small moment arrows by default and confused players; a later version hid the confusing ones by default, leaving only lift/drag on control surfaces visible unless the player opts into more. [FAR GitHub](https://github.com/ferram4/Ferram-Aerospace-Research). **Direct lesson for Sunscatter's Structural layer's network lines (`rules::networks`) and any future aero-force visualization:** default to the minimum arrow set that answers "is something about to break," let a deeper click reveal the full force breakdown — i.e. the systems screen's existing "networks drawn as gizmo lines, inspector for full detail on click" pattern already avoids FAR's mistake, as long as new overlays are built with that same default-minimal discipline rather than drawing every vector FAR's early version did.

**Connected Living Space:** a stock-adjacent mod that computes and visualizes which *crewed* parts are pressurized/connected to each other (for EVA-without-depressurizing purposes) as a distinct derived network over the part graph. Relevant precedent for a **future** "life support / pressurized volume" layer once crew systems grow (`ship-systems.md`'s crew/cabin section is already playable but single-cabin; a multi-module craft, e.g. a station, would need exactly this kind of connectivity-derived layer) — not needed for the Falcon 9 Crew's one cabin today, but worth a line in `systems-screen.md`'s "not done" list for when a multi-module craft exists.

**No contradiction of the realism target found in this topic** — Sunscatter's systems screen is already ahead of every surveyed mod on the core question (showing the *real* craft's parts per layer rather than a hand-drawn diagram); the two additions worth raising are both strict improvements (thermal rate-of-change, a future connectivity layer), not simplifications.

## 8. Mac keyboard

Confirmed mechanism: macOS F-keys default to system media/brightness functions and require holding `fn` to send the literal F-key code to an app, unless the user has flipped System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Function Keys → "Use F1, F2, etc. as standard function keys" — a *per-user* setting the game cannot assume or set for them. [Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-keyboard-function-keys-mchlp2596/mac), [OSXDaily](https://osxdaily.com/2020/02/11/what-do-f1-f12-f-keys-do-mac-keyboard/). Sunscatter's key help (`interface/help.rs`) already leans heavily on F2–F9 for the "Game" group (tracking station, quicksave/load, saves, settings, perf overlay, spawn test ships, help) — **every one of those is subject to the same fragility as F8**, not just F8; F8 is simply the one the owner has personally hit (likely because F8 on current macOS defaults to Mission Control / a media key many laptops intercept at the OS level even with the "standard function keys" setting on, depending on model). This is worth broadening: the fix should not be "move F8," it should be "stop anchoring any frequently-used binding to a bare F-key on a project that ships on macOS," per the project's own commands (CLAUDE.md: macOS and Windows are both CI-tested platforms).

**Convention other games use instead:** letter keys with no modifier for frequent actions (exactly what Sunscatter already does for flight — O, L, U, G, N, T, P, Space — none of the *flight* keys are F-keys, only the "Game" utility group is), and reserve F-keys (if used at all) for rare, non-time-critical actions where an occasional extra `fn` press is tolerable (quicksave, settings, help). Tab is a near-universal convention for "toggle a secondary full-screen view" (inventory, map, scoreboard) across PC games and is unused on Mac at the OS level in a way that would intercept it.

**Recommendation:**
- Move the **systems screen** off F8 onto **Tab** (free, not in `RESERVED`, matches the cross-game convention for "toggle a secondary full view," and is not vulnerable to the fn-key issue at all).
- Leave F8 bound as a secondary/legacy alias if cheap to keep (some players will have muscle memory), but Tab becomes primary and the one documented in the help overlay.
- Flag F5/F6/F7/F9/F2–F4 as lower-risk only because they're infrequent actions, but note in Open Questions that the owner may want the same treatment for any of them if Windows/Mac parity keeps biting.

## 9. Scripting integration hooks (brief)

**kOS:** staging state is exposed structurally (`Stage:READY`, `Stage:NUMBER`, `Stage:RESOURCES`) and engines expose `ENGINE:IGNITIONS`, so a script can read exactly the same "is this stage still good, how much is left" question the UI is being asked to answer visually. [kOS Stage docs](https://ksp-kos.github.io/KOS/structures/vessels/stage.html), [kOS Engine docs](https://ksp-kos.github.io/KOS/structures/vessels/engine.html). **This is strong validation that Sunscatter's stage bar and part cards should be *views onto* the same `Knowledge`/command data model D106 and D108 already mandate scripts will read** — i.e. the stage bar's per-part icon state and the part card's rows should be computed from the same `sim::systems::data::Knowledge` API a future script would call, not a UI-only recomputation. Building the stage bar against that API now (even before scripting lands) means scripting "sees" exactly what the player's eye sees later, free of charge, and avoids a second owner of "what does this stage's state mean" (rule 5).
**kRPC:** does the same via a general-purpose RPC surface rather than an in-game terminal, with no screen-specific lesson beyond confirming the same principle (one data model, multiple consumers: UI, MCP, scripts) that Sunscatter's `commands::route` and D108 already commit to.

No action needed beyond: when building the stage bar / part card, read from `Knowledge` (or whatever its stage-level successor is) rather than re-deriving display strings ad hoc in `game`, so the eventual script surface and the MCP server get this for free.

## Proposed part-card content (per kind)

Every row obeys D106 (dash if unsensed) and D105 (shown against the part's own rated/limit number, never a bare value with no comparison where a limit exists). Header: part name, catalog entry, a link-button to "open in systems screen" (satisfies "anything more is a link to a specific screen").

- **Engine:** state (off/starting/running/shutting down/failed), throttle %, thrust / rated thrust (vac or current ambient, whichever applies), Isp now, propellant feed status (settled/floating/cavitating), ignitions remaining / rated, gimbal angle / limit, temperature / rated-destruction pair.
- **Tank:** contents (fluid name), level kg / capacity kg (D106 dash if no level sensor), ullage pressure / MEOP, propellant temperature, settled/floating flag.
- **Battery:** charge kg·Wh or % / capacity, charge/discharge rate, temperature, cell count if failures are per-cell.
- **Decoupler/separation bolt:** attached/separated, armed/safed if applicable, no further live stats (it's a binary-state part — a short card, which is fine, not every part needs five rows).
- **Legs:** stowed/deploying/deployed angle vs target, crush-core stroke used / available, per-leg load if sensed.
- **Grid fins:** deflection angle / limit, deployed/stowed, actuator rate, (no heating readout yet per `ship-systems.md`'s "not done": grid-fin heating).
- **RCS thruster (block):** on/off (function/power/feed), current commanded force this tick, propellant flow, gas remaining in its feed.
- **Heat shield / ablator-bearing part:** skin temperature / rated-destruction, ablator remaining (if modelled as consumable mass) / initial, char/erosion state if any.
- **Crew module:** cabin pressure, O₂/CO₂ partial pressures / alarm thresholds, crew g-load against the NASA-STD-3001 axis limit currently binding (same number as §4's navball readout, same owner, shown here per-occupant context), life-support powered/unpowered.
- **Solar panel / servo mechanism (generic):** deployed angle/target, actuator rate, power output now (if a solar cell), cycles used / rated.
- **Sensor/computer component:** linked/not linked, powered/lost, what it feeds (a one-line pointer, not a duplicate of the network view already in the systems screen).

## Open questions for the owner

1. **IJKL/HN collision.** The owner's "IJKL translate" (and the implied KSP H/N forward/back convention) directly shadows three existing single-letter bindings: `L` (Landing/Reentry menu), `H` (help overlay), `N` (burn planner). Options: (a) only bind I/J/K/L and skip H/N forward/back (use a different pair, or fold forward/back into the throttle axis while RCS mode is on), (b) let RCS-mode-on *temporarily* shadow L/H/N (acceptable if those three menus can still be opened via their dock/menu-bar buttons, not only their key — true today per `flight-menus.md`'s menu bar), or (c) pick different letters entirely for translation. Recommend (b): matches real HOTAS practice (mode-dependent key meaning) and needs no relearning of KSP muscle memory, since the Orbital/Landing/Utilities buttons in the menu bar remain clickable during RCS mode.
2. **RCS toggle key.** Confirm a letter for it; KSP's own `R` is taken by reset here. Free letters today: B, C, M, V, Y (plus digits 7/8/9/0, which the mini-panel already treats as its own overflow pool — picking one of those would collide with a craft's pinned-function key assignment under load, so a *letter* is safer).
3. **Engine "Starting" transient vs. warp gate.** Should rails/physics warp be refused only once an engine is fully `Running`, or already during its start transient (2 s Merlin spin-up, etc., which does produce some thrust)? Affects the exact state-machine hook for the new warp-refusal rule.
4. **Sensor-starved stage bar.** If a stage's tanks have no level sensor, should the stage bar show "—" for fill and time (strict D106), or a softer "last known" / "estimated from mass budget" indicator? No prior art surveyed models this (every KSP mod assumes omniscient fuel gauging) — this is new ground Sunscatter would be first to walk, worth the owner's explicit call since it affects how useful the bar is on a minimally-instrumented craft.
5. **Which "max g" for an uncrewed vessel.** Confirmed structural-joint-ratio fallback when no crew limit applies (§4) — does the owner want a different "max" for an uncrewed craft (e.g. the weakest *rated* part regardless of current knowledge, shown as a dash-qualified estimate) or is "whatever the ship can currently sense" the right default, consistent with D106?
6. **Thermal rate-of-change.** Worth adding a heating/cooling trend indicator (Hot Spot's lesson, §7) to the thermal layer and Landing/Reentry's SKIN MAX row now, or defer?
7. **F-key breadth.** Is Tab-for-systems-screen sufficient, or should the owner want the same anti-F-key treatment extended to F2–F9 now rather than piecemeal later?
8. **Part-card persistence scope.** "One constant menu per part" — confirm this means one consistent *template* per part kind (recommended reading, §2) rather than literally one single on-screen window slot shared by all parts.
