# Interview 2026-10-30 (night): planned burns, warp, the software layers

Ten questions from the lead before PLANNER, ONEKIND-3 and the sim-wide wave (the hand-off's next steps 1–3). The owner's answers are verbatim where they wrote their own. Decisions: D126 and D120 revised, D128 and D129 new, D063 and D121 extended.

## Answers

1. **How a planned burn on a craft of parts runs under warp.** "Burns are live, unless they are integrated into the timewarp systems as a special case, which will come later with the warp while thrusting setup. I hope this demonstrates part of what the inactive timewarp systems must look like: deterministic, coarse grained or analytic, don't change behavior across warp levels. EXACTLY the same behavior at 100,000 warp as you would get at 1x warp. That is part of determinism. Tell me where that is difficult, because I would like that to be the goal, and for that to define how we restrict fast timewarping and simulate systems under warp. Deterministic course of events is only broken when the player inputs something. Failures, for example, don't pause timewarp unless they are critical and in general not much should fail during cruise when a ship is not being actively flown. For having dozens of crafts doing trade routes, there should also be an option to turn off notifications for a craft: if something fails, it fails, the autopilot scripts do the best they can. Anyways, generally repeat missions will rarely have a critical failure."
2. **What the player sets as a planned burn:** nodes only (Δv prograde, normal and radial at a time), closed-loop to the orbit the node gives. Orbit targets and rendezvous come with the Dragon flight.
3. **Settling before ignition:** "1 for auto burns, and the player can always do all that manually." An automatic burn settles its propellant with the craft's own thrusters, as the Falcon 9's second stage does.
4. **Manual input during a planned burn:** "It should be a full switch ONLY when input disagrees/when it would interfere."
5. **The playtest save:** "Dragon alone in orbit, but no need to restrict to one save. Make as many saves as you want for debugging (they should be compact in format, relying on the determinism)."
6. **The abort's escape-thrust check:** "This does not need to be a complex system. Literally just if a LES system exists for this purpose, the player can fire it at any time, and it always runs the same. If the ship is being flown automatically, the on board script might check [a thrust-to-weight margin] and make a decision. No [predicted escape] needed."
7. **PERF's scope:** "Game too, but I don't want to steer you towards chasing some target of 5000 cubes arbitrarily rendered. We will be making optimizations for many part crafts in the future, but we don't even know what those crafts will look like. Use your judgement."
8. **What stops warp** (the lead offered: crew or vessel at risk; mission-ending only; any failure). "1 as default when there is the bare minimum of software on the ship. That bare minimum software point should be well defined, I don't want "ship computers" mingling until assembling a ship means fighting an integration of procedures. Each part should have built in, fundamental logic. Technically "run" by the ship's "computer" in our abstraction, but that fundamental logic is built into each part, synonomous with how the part actually works. If the ship just has this, it would be theoretically possible to control the ship manually, but nearly impossible. You would have to dig into menus and regulate atmosphere flow and similar. Then there is the ship level computer, layer 2. It makes pretty much the same inputs the player makes, but with simple hard coded logic, plus some elements that are more automated. A manually driven ship will usually have elements of layer 2 running in the background, and elements of layer 2 (such as actions/functions) can be activated by the player, performing sequences of actions. If coded well, layer 2 is capable of running whole well-defined missions start to finish, and will typically have logic built in to handle errors, although the versatility is based on how much effort you put into your layer 2. Some elements of layer 2 are isolated functions/files that can be shipped with the craft (like booster landing loops). Some things are more integrated and use multiple functions, for example you could have a central script with edge cases to handle errors that calls the maneuver function or the landing function. These pieces of software should be sharable just like crafts. Finally we have the AI agent, external to the game, that has paths to interact directly with the ship computer easily (although the human player can also do that in a special "computer" tab), plus the ability to call functions, take screenshots, use the controls itself, etc. Hope that makes sense. Back to the original question, there should be a built in line for stopping warp for a ship with bare minimum built in part functionality (which should be enough to coast for months if everything is set up, even with no layer 2 set up at all). However, a good layer 2 can handle many things so that the line for stopping warp can be entirely decided by whoever programmed layer 2. In general: fundamental level of software at the bottom, typical baseline software that generally comes with a ship or even is added to a part is in the middle and we will ship the game with it and players typically share this software along with a craft, and a complex layer 2 system to automatically run a mission between the earth and mars is far more complex but possible, requiring reliable parts and still running the risk of failure, especially without human intervention."
9. **When the code is split into the layers:** in the wave, by a LAYERS agent before FIXES3. PLANNER writes its burn as a layer-2 function.
10. **What a save holds:** "I didn't mean THAT much rebuilding. Don't make things harder than you need to. I meant stuff like planetary data and part behavior data don't need to be held in a save file. This comment was kind of random. A ship's trajectory can still be held in a save file, thermal state should DEFINITELY be held, just choose whatever is easiest."

## Where "the same at 100,000× as at 1×" is hard (the lead's first answer; AUDIT checks it)

The sim already picks a craft's model from its state, not from the warp. A craft goes live when it thrusts, enters the air, touches the ground or is near a live craft, and coasts otherwise, even at 1×. So the two models never switch because the player changed warp. What is hard:

1. **Cost, not determinism.** A live craft ticks every 20 ms, so while anything is live (a burn, an entry, a landing), rails warp is refused for every craft. With dozens of craft on trade routes, someone is often burning, and warp keeps dropping. D029's warp while thrusting is the fix: a burn integrated into the warp systems with the same law.
2. **The last bits across frames.** Each warp level advances by a different span per frame. Every law must step on a lattice fixed to absolute time, or be evaluated in closed form at any epoch, and never from "the time since the last frame". Several laws are tested for this (chunked = single pass); ADVANCE has to make all of them so and test each one at 1× against 10⁵×.
3. **Events inside a frame.** A failure, a relief valve lifting, a battery running flat or an atmosphere entry must be found at its own epoch, not at the end of the frame that contains it. Where a coarse law finds it only on its 60 s lattice, it lands on the lattice: the same at every warp, but coarser than live.
4. **Stopping warp.** Warp has to stop at the critical event's epoch, with the craft state at that epoch, whatever the warp was.
5. **Ship programs under warp.** A layer-2 program has to run on its own fixed schedule of epochs and read only what the craft knows. A program that decides to burn makes its craft live, and so caps warp, until warp while thrusting exists.
6. **The player's input** lands at the epoch of the frame it arrives in, which is coarser at high warp. That is the one accepted break (answer 1).
